01

Brief.

Euangelion calls itself "a daily newspaper of the Gospel." The visual language already does broadsheet impressively well — masthead, dateline, kickers, folios, hairline rules, bottom-brand block. What's missing is the front-page feeling: density, surprise, a small daily ritual that says "today's edition is its own thing."

Two anchors decided already: add THE FUNNIES (tone confirmed: "Peanuts but Jesus") and adopt a bento layout (a broadsheet front page is, structurally, a bento). The founder also asked for interaction polish — cinemagraphs, scroll-driven reveals, Awwwards-tier feel — without sliding into gimmick.

This page presents six sample funnies captions to react to, a two-sided argument for the LETTERS TO THE EDITOR sub-kicker, a bento tile gamut (the obvious + the wildcards), and an interaction gamut (subtle motion → page-turn dreams). Each card has effort, risk, and a ship-in-v1 call so the founder can move through it sequentially.

Nothing on this page propagates anywhere else on the site. It is a review document.

02 · The Funnies

"Peanuts but Jesus" — calibrating the voice.

Schulz wrote 560+ Peanuts strips with religious content over fifty years. Linus was his spiritual voice. The voice was never preachy and never snarky — it held hope and skepticism in the same panel. That's the bar.

What "Peanuts but Jesus" means in practice.

  • Honest about hard things, said by a small voice. Charlie-Brown energy: failure as the default state, grace as the surprise.
  • One sentence, max two. A panel doesn't argue. It observes.
  • Reverent without sentimentality. Sally asking "When we die, will we go to Heaven?" and Charlie answering "I like to think so" — that's the register. Not preachy. Not flippant.
  • Never punching down. Job's friends are fair game; Job isn't.
  • Image leads, caption tags. A single woodcut panel + one quiet line under it. No comic-strip word balloons, no multi-panel sequences in v1.

Six sample captions — which voice lands?

Each panel reuses an existing print from archive/devotional-prints/. Pick the ones that feel right; cross out the ones that don't. These six span tones from quiet-philosophical to wry-confessional so we can find the center of gravity.

Image-selection criteria.

  1. Always from the curated archive. The 643 archive prints in archive/devotional-prints/ are already brand-aligned (cobalt + cream + crimson halftone). No new generation.
  2. Single subject reads cleanly small. Funnies tile is the smallest in the bento — anything busy gets visually lost. Vermeer, Chardin, single figures, single objects.
  3. No embedded text in the image. The caption is the caption.
  4. Image should leave room for the caption to land. A face mid-action, a figure walking — not a frozen idea diagram.

Authoring scale.

20 entries to seed (≈ three weeks at one-a-day). Daily rotation via daysSinceEpoch % funnies.length. Adding more is one entry in src/data/funnies.ts; no schema change. The founder is the editorial voice; I write drafts, founder approves.

03 · Soul Audit kicker

Should Soul Audit get a LETTERS TO THE EDITOR sub-kicker?

A small italic byline above the existing SOUL AUDIT kicker. Three-word change. Reinforces the newspaper voice — or quietly undermines a section that was just reverted to default styling.

PRO — Ship the sub-kicker.

It doubles down on the metaphor where it matters. Soul Audit is the section most exposed to "this is software" framing (textarea, prompt pills, submit button). Calling it LETTERS TO THE EDITOR reframes the same UI as writing in to the paper — closer to a confessional than a form.

It pairs the section with the bento LETTERS tile. If we ship a LETTERS tile in the front-page bento that funnels here, the two pieces share a name and a behavior. Reader writes a letter; the editor (Soul Audit) reads it.

It's a three-word change, reversible in 30 seconds. No content rewrite, no layout shift, no styling change. Pure copy.

CON — Leave Soul Audit alone.

The section just got reverted to default palette (R39, R42). Adding another layer of theming right after stripping one creates a yo-yo signal. The section now wants to recede visually, not pop.

"SOUL AUDIT" is already a strong kicker. It's named for what it does. Reframing it as a letter to the editor reads cute on a marketing page, then strains every time a returning user looks at it as an actual tool. Cuteness has a shelf life.

The metaphor is doing enough already. Dateline, masthead, kickers, folios, bento, funnies — adding a sub-kicker here is the kind of detail that makes a page feel over-themed. Restraint is a feature.

Recommendation

Ship the LETTERS bento tile that funnels into Soul Audit; do NOT add a sub-kicker on Soul Audit itself. The metaphor lands in the bento (small, framed, repeatable). The Soul Audit section stays the working tool. Two surfaces, two voices. The founder keeps the metaphor without paying a tax on the most-used CTA.

Suggested v1 bento composition.

The minimum that feels like a front page without overwhelming a first-time reader:

  1. Lead Headline — anchor, 8 × 4
  2. Today's Scripture — small, 4 × 2
  3. Opinion — small, 4 × 2
  4. The Funnies — small, 4 × 2
  5. Letters — wide tag, 8 × 2
  6. Editor's Desk — medium, 5 × 2
  7. Almanac — narrow, 3 × 2

That's 7 tiles, mobile-collapses to single column. Add Jump-line as a typographic detail inside the Lead Headline tile (not its own tile). Wildcards (Weather, Obituaries, Crossword, Marginalia, Sports, Travel, Grapevine, Errata) all stay in the data layer — they can be turned on as additional tiles in later rounds without restructuring the grid.

05 · Motion

Interaction + animation — small touches through cinematic moments.

Subtle motion is the 2026 baseline; CSS scroll-driven animations now ship in every browser without JS. Below: every idea worth distilling, ordered roughly by effort. The point is breadth — pick the few that fit. Each lists effort, risk, and a sketch of what it'd look like.

Group A — Founder-named candidates.

Roll-the-Stone Hero

M

As the reader scrolls, the stone slides off the tomb. Light comes through.

The existing blue-tomb hero (hero/header-v2.webp) becomes the still frame. A second layer — the stone — translates X across the scroll-distance of the hero section. By the time the user passes the hero, the tomb is open.

Pure CSS via animation-timeline: view(). No JS, no GSAP. Honours prefers-reduced-motion trivially. Needs the existing hero file split into stone + tomb layers; could be done in-CSS with mask or as two PNGs.

risk reads cinematic; could read cute if the timing is off — needs careful easingtheology first impression of the site = empty tomb. Strong.
Ship v1

A single letter in EUANGELION pulses like a heartbeat.

Subtle. Very subtle. The "E" or the second "E" gets a 6-second opacity cycle from 100% to 92%. Almost subliminal. The page feels breathing without anything moving.

06 · Recommendations

What I'd ship in v1 — given your tastes.

Bento tiles: Lead Headline · Today's Scripture · The Funnies · Opinion · Letters · Editor's Desk · Almanac. Seven tiles. Jump-line as a typographic detail inside the lead, not its own tile.

Funnies authoring: 20 entries in the "Peanuts but Jesus" voice, seeded from the six samples above (pending your edits). Daily rotation by date.
