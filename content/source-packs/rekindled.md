## 2. LEXICON — verified against BibleHub interlinear + Strong's

### The spine: one verb, three states

| Text      | Form                            | Sense                     |
| --------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Lev 6:13  | לֹא תִכְבֶה — _lo tikbeh_       | it shall **not** go out   |
| 1 Sam 3:3 | טֶרֶם יִכְבֶּה — _terem yikbeh_ | had **not yet** gone out  |
| Matt 25:8 | σβέννυνται — _sbennyntai_       | **are** going out         |
| Isa 42:3  | לֹא יְכַבֶּנָּה — _kabah_       | he will **not quench** it |

Root **כָּבָה (_kabah_), H3518** — "be quenched, extinguished, go out," specifically of fire or lamp. **σβέννυμι (_sbennymi_), G4570** is the Septuagint's standard rendering of _kabah_ (Thayer). So Matthew 25:8 and Leviticus 6:13 are lexically the same idea across both testaments.

**The command and the promise are the same word.** What we are told not to let go out (Lev 6:13) is what He promises not to quench (Isa 42:3). This is the series' resolution and it is textual, not homiletical.

### בְּרֵאשִׁית — _bereshit_, Genesis 1:1

Letters in order: ב · ר · א · שׁ · י · ת

- Outer four (1,2 + 5,6) → **בְּרִית** (_b'rit_), **H1285**, covenant. BDB derives it from a root meaning _to cut_ — "a compact, because made by passing between pieces of flesh."
- Inner two (3,4) → **אֵשׁ** (_esh_), **H784**, fire. A primitive word, no root derivation.

**Actual etymology:** בְּ (_b'_, "in") + רֵאשִׁית (_reshit_, **H7225**, "beginning"), from רֹאשׁ (_rosh_, **H7218**, "head"). The pattern **cuts across three morphemes** — preposition, root, suffix. No morphological analysis produces that division. The א in _reshit_ is quiescent; the י in both words is a _mater lectionis_.

**ATTESTED — Midrash Aggadah (ed. Buber, Vienna 1894), Genesis 1:1:6.** Verbatim: _bet, resh — rosh ha-milah_; _yod, tav — acharit ha-milah_; _v'hu b'rit_. Then: _ki nish'ar mi-milat bereshit a"sh v'hu esh_ — "for there remains from the word _bereshit_ aleph-shin, and that is fire." https://www.sefaria.org/Midrash_Aggadah,_Genesis.1.1.6

**MANDATORY CONSTRAINTS:**

1. Call it **a medieval midrash.** NOT "the ancient rabbis," NOT the Talmud. Buber recovered it from an Aleppo manuscript, first published 1894.
2. The midrash reads _b'rit_ as **circumcision and Sabbath**, and _esh_ as **Torah** ("there is no fire but Torah," Deut 33:2). It is about why the world was created. It is **NOT** about the Genesis 15 covenant of fire. Our application is ours — say so.
3. 🚩 **The Baal HaTurim attribution circulating online is FALSE.** His actual Genesis 1:1 comments give gematria, notarikon, and a _b'rit_-and-Torah acronym — but not this letter split. Do not cite him.

### ἀναζωπυρεῖν — _anazōpyrein_, 2 Timothy 1:6

**G329**, lexical form ἀναζωπυρέω. **Present Infinitive Active.** **Hapax legomenon** — one NT occurrence.
Etymology: ἀνά (G303, "again/anew") + ζω- (living) + πῦρ (G4442, fire). Thayer derives it from τὸ ζώπυρον — **"embers," "that by which the fire is kindled anew."**

**The fire pre-exists — three independent lines:**

1. **Lexical:** ζώπυρον = embers. Live coals under ash.
2. **LXX:** Genesis 45:27, "the spirit of Jacob their father **revived**." He was alive and despondent; what revived pre-existed. Also 1 Macc 13:7.
3. **Grammar — lead with this:** Paul says fan into flame the gift **ὅ ἐστιν ἐν σοί** (_ho estin en soi_), "which **is** in you." Present indicative. Existence asserted by the verse, not inferred from the verb.

**Concession that costs nothing:** ἀνα- compounds can be _intensive_ ("keep at full flame") as well as _iterative_ ("kindle again"). **Both readings require a pre-existing fire.** Concede it openly; it closes the only lexical door a critic has.

### Genesis 15 — the torch, not the lamp

**לַפִּיד אֵשׁ (_lappid esh_), H3940 + H784** — construct chain, literally **"torch of fire."** _Lappid_ is Gideon's torches in jars (Judg 7:16,20), torches on foxes' tails (Judg 15:4-5), lightning (Nah 2:4). BDB: "a torch used for portable light during night travel." **NAS never renders it "lamp."**

⚠️ **KJV's "burning lamp" is straining.** _Lappid_ is a different object from **נֵר (_ner_), H5216** — the small clay household oil lamp of Exodus 27:20 and 1 Samuel 3:3.

**Use the distinction, don't flatten it:**

- **TORCH (_lappid_)** = what God does alone. Blazing, hand-carried, sovereign. Genesis 15.
- **LAMP (_ner_)** = what we are given to keep. Small, fed, tended. Exodus 27, 1 Samuel 3.

"Smoking furnace" = תַנּוּר עָשָׁן (_tannur ashan_), **H8574** — a portable clay **firepot/oven**, not an industrial furnace.

### תַּרְדֵּמָה — _tardemah_, Genesis 15:12

**H8639**, "deep sleep." **Same word as Genesis 2:21** (Adam). 7 occurrences: Gen 2:21, Gen 15:12, 1 Sam 26:12, Job 4:13, Job 33:15, Prov 19:15, Isa 29:10.

Pattern: excluding Prov 19:15, every occurrence involves divine initiative and human passivity — and in Gen 2:21, Gen 15:12, Job 4:13 and 33:15, something is _made_ or _revealed_ while the person cannot act.

⚠️ **Grammar caveat:** Gen 2:21 has וַיַּפֵּל (Hiphil, "He caused to fall") with God as explicit subject. Gen 15:12 has נָפְלָה (Qal, "fell") — **God is not the grammatical agent.** Write "a deep sleep fell on Abram" and let the Adam parallel carry it.

### Leviticus 6:13 — the gift in the verb stem

אֵשׁ תָּמִיד תּוּקַד עַל־הַמִּזְבֵּחַ לֹא תִכְבֶה — _esh tamid tuqad al-hammizbeach lo tikbeh_

**תּוּקַד (_tuqad_) is Hophal — causative passive.** Not "the fire burns" but "**the fire is caused to be kept burning**." Human agency is encoded in the verb stem.

⚠️ **_tamid_ (H8548) is not always "unceasing."** In cultic contexts it often means "regular / at appointed times" — the _tamid_ offering was twice daily. In Lev 6:12-13 specifically the context forces the strong sense, because the text adds _lo tikbeh_. Do not generalise.

📎 English Leviticus 6:13 = **Hebrew Leviticus 6:6.**

### Exodus 27:20 — priestly light, congregational fuel

_v'attah t'tzavveh et-b'nei Yisrael v'yiqchu eleikha_ — "you shall command **the sons of Israel**, that **they bring to you**" pure beaten olive oil. **תָּמִיד — same word and Strong's number as Lev 6:13.**

⚠️ **Precision:** the community's duty is to **SUPPLY** the oil. The **tending is priestly** (v. 21, Aaron and his sons, evening to morning). Correct phrasing: _"the light is priestly, but the fuel is congregational — the flame in the sanctuary burns on oil that ordinary households pressed and carried in."_

### Matthew 25:8 — soften the lamp claim

σβέννυνται — **Present Indicative Middle/Passive, 3rd plural.** "Are going out" is correct; not perfect, not aorist. The lamps are actively dying, still alight.

⚠️ **λαμπάς (G2985) is probably not a _ner_.** The NT's ordinary word for a household lamp is **λύχνος** (Matt 5:15, the lamp under the basket) — and _lychnos_ is also the LXX's rendering of _ner_. John 18:3 uses _lampas_ of torches carried at night. **Safe framing:** "_lampas_ — the word for a light carried out into the dark, whether torch or oil lamp." True on either reading.

## 3. HISTORICAL CONTEXT — honest ranges

- **Genesis 15 self-curse:** the dominant reading, but an **inference**. Genesis 15 contains **no curse formula at all**. **Jeremiah 34:18 is where the logic is explicit** — play that card. ANE evidence: cutting animals at treaty-making is well attested (Sefire, Ashurnirari V); **walking between the halves is close to biblically unique.**
- **The menorah did NOT burn 24/7.** The lamp text says "evening until morning" and never carries the altar's _lo tikbeh_. 1 Samuel 3:3 uses the lamp's extinction as a **clock**. This strengthens day 5.
- **Synagogue _ner tamid_ is substantially a retrojection** — absent from Mishnah, Talmud, Maimonides and Shulhan Arukh; first attested 17th century. Claim no continuity.
- **2 Timothy authorship:** a 2011 survey of 111 BNTC scholars ran **63 for authenticity, 13 against.** The flat "most scholars reject the Pastorals" is overstated. Note the debate; don't pick a fight.
- **"Our lamps are going out" is a slow fade**, and this holds on either the torch or lamp reading — no need to resolve it.

## 4. STORIES — VERIFIED

### John Newton (1725-1807) — LEAD STORY, fully public domain

The fire was lit by his mother, who **died thirteen days before his seventh birthday.** It went out for **sixteen years**. It took another **thirty-four** to finish burning through him. And by his own written admission **he continued captaining slave ships for six years after the 1748 storm** normally treated as his conversion.

Why it leads: it is the unsentimental version. Rekindling here is slow, partial, and embarrassing in the middle. His own words, no copyright cost.

### Sojourner Truth / Isabella Baumfree (c.1797-1883) — VERIFIED, public domain worldwide

**Primary:** Olive Gilbert, _Narrative of Sojourner Truth_, Boston, 1850. Gutenberg #1674.

**THE FINDING:** her faith went out **in comfort, not in suffering.** She prayed hardest when most brutalised; safety at the Van Wageners' extinguished it:

> "Here, at Mr. Van Wagener's… she was so happy and satisfied, that God was entirely forgotten. Why should her thoughts turn to him, who was only known to her as a help in trouble?"

Sermons invariably invert this. For a series about a fire gone dim, it is the whole point.

The inheritance, taught in Low Dutch under the night sky by a mother weeping for children already sold:

> "'My children, there is a God, who hears and sees you.' 'A God, mau-mau! Where does he live?' asked the children. 'He lives in the sky,' she replied; 'and when you are beaten, or cruelly treated, or fall into any trouble, you must ask help of him, and he will always hear and help you.' She taught them to kneel and say the Lord's Prayer."

Surviving into adulthood:

> "She would sometimes repeat, 'Our Father in heaven,' in her Low Dutch, as taught her by her mother"

⚠️ **PROVENANCE — must travel with every quotation.** Truth could not read or write. Gilbert wrote the book in the third person from Truth's spoken account. Three tiers:

1. **Gilbert's narration** — "Thus perished the inward spark, like a flame just igniting…" **This is GILBERT's metaphor, not Truth's.** If the day uses it, the day must say so.
2. **Reported speech** — "'Oh, God, I did not know you were so big'"; "'Now the war begun.'" Closest to her voice, still filtered.
3. **Flagged translations** — the mother's teaching, which Gilbert marks "ran nearly as follows." Double mediation: Low Dutch → decades of memory → Gilbert's English.

Correct claim: **her authorized account** (published in her lifetime, sold by her at lectures for 33 years) — not "her words."

⚠️ Other traps: it is not an autobiography; **no year is given for the vision** (1827 is scholars' dating); the text says outright it cannot date her mother's death; she was **Isabella** then — "Sojourner Truth" was taken in 1843; **"Ain't I a Woman" is not in this Narrative.** The mother's teaching is survival instruction, not a bedtime scene — keep the edge or it goes false.

### C. S. Lewis (1898-1963) — VERIFIED, dating DISPUTED, copyright-limited

Mother Flora died **23 August 1908**, aged 46 (Belfast City Council burial register, plot D/637). Lewis was nine. He had prayed for her healing "without love, without awe," approaching God "merely as a magician."

**The usable finding: Lewis could not reliably date his own relighting.** He said Trinity Term 1929 and repeated it as late as 1962. McGrath dates it 27 Apr–21 Jun 1930; Lazo 1–10 June 1930; Heck 17–26 July 1930. **The three who agree he was wrong cannot agree with each other.** Published dissent: Greg Forster, _First Things_, 11 March 2013.

⚠️ **COPYRIGHT: in copyright until 2034 (UK/EU) and into the 2050s (US).** Short cited phrases only, always with chapter and page. Verified phrases from _Surprised by Joy_ (1956 Harcourt 1st US): "without love, without awe" / "merely as a magician" (ch. I p.18); "all settled happiness, all that was tranquil and reliable" (ch. I p.19); "ceased to be a Christian. The chronology of this disaster is a little vague" (ch. IV p.56); "the most dejected and reluctant convert in all England" (ch. XIV p.221).

🚩 **Do NOT quote the 1916 "all religions… are merely man's own invention" line.** Letter date and subject verified; the wording appears only on blogs and devotional sites.

### Monica and Augustine — VERIFIED (day 5)

**Translations, both public domain:** J. G. Pilkington (1886, NPNF vol. I) and E. B. Pusey (1838, Library of the Fathers). Latin checked against thelatinlibrary.com.

**THE ANCHOR QUOTE — _Confessions_ V.viii.15, Pilkington:**

> "But Thou, mysteriously counselling and hearing the real purpose of her desire, granted not what she then asked, in order to make me what she was ever asking."

⚠️ **The popular form is a PARAPHRASE, not a quotation.** "He denied her what she asked that day in order to grant what she had always asked" is **not the wording of any public-domain translation.** Neither Pusey nor Pilkington uses "denied" or "that day." Print Pilkington in quotation marks, or print the modern cadence as an unquoted paraphrase beside it. Never both.

⭐ **The Latin carries an image neither translation keeps:** _cardinem desiderii eius_ — **_cardo_ is a hinge.** God heard the **hinge of her desire**, the thing everything else swung on, and disregarded what she was actually saying. That is day 5 in one word, defensible straight from the Latin.

**The Carthage deception — V.viii.15.** He lied: he claimed a friend he could not leave until the wind turned. Latin: _et mentitus sum matri, et illi matri_ — "and I lied to my mother — and such a mother!" She spent the night at a **shrine of Cyprian** (_memoria beati Cypriani_) beside the harbour, not a church.

⚠️ **The popular version is wrong twice.** She did **not** weep nobly in silence — she **accused him**: "after accusing my perfidy and cruelty." And she did **not** follow his ship. She "**returned to her accustomed place**." She crossed to Italy later, to Milan (VI.i.1). Any retelling that has her chasing him across the Mediterranean that week is invented.

**"The son of these tears" — III.xii.21.** Both translations identical:

> "Go thy way, and God bless thee, for it is not possible that the son of these tears should perish."

⚠️ **The bishop is NOT NAMED** — "a certain bishop." Any devotional that names him is inventing.
⚠️ **"Impossible" is a paraphrase.** _Fieri non potest._ Both PD translations read "it is not possible."
⭐ **He was irritated.** He had already **refused** to meet Augustine. Latin _substomachans taedio_ — irritated to disgust — and the sentence **opens with _vade a me_, "go away from me."** The most-quoted consolation in Christian history was a brush-off from a man trying to get a persistent woman out of his study. Monica took it "as though it were a voice from heaven."

**Ostia — IX.x.26, Pusey:**

> "One thing there was for which I desired to linger for a while in this life, that I might see thee a Catholic Christian before I died."

Her last instruction (IX.xi):

> "Lay this body any where; let not the care for that any way disquiet you: this only I request, that you would remember me at the Lord's altar, wherever you be."
> "Nothing is far to God."

**Timeline — with reconstructions flagged:**

- **~17 years** of named, anguished intercession: from his sixteenth year (II.iii.7) to baptism at 32. ⚠️ **Do NOT say "thirty years."** The "almost nine years" of III.xi.19 is the **Manichee span**, not the span of her praying.
- **Baptism: Easter 387, Milan.** ⚠️ The _Confessions_ **does not name the baptiser** (IX.vi.14: "And we were baptised"). **Ambrose comes from Possidius**, _Vita Augustini_ I, who says _through_ Ambrose. Say "Easter 387," not a calendar date.
- **Death: Ostia, in her fifty-sixth year** (so strictly 55), Augustine 32 — therefore **before 13 Nov 387**, roughly 5–6 months after his baptism. ⚠️ **REJECT "27 August 387"** — a liturgical date assigned in the 1969 calendar revision to sit before Augustine's feast. Not a documented death date.

**Manichaeism — nine years** (IV.i.1: "from my nineteenth year of age to my twenty-eighth"). He was a **hearer (_auditor_), never one of the Elect** — proven by IV.i.1 and V.x.18 in his own words.

⭐ **The best line, V.x.18 (Pusey):**

> "it delighted my pride, to be free from blame; and when I had done any evil, not to confess I had done any… But in truth it was wholly I, and mine impiety had divided me against myself: and that sin was the more incurable, whereby I did not judge myself a sinner."

The system's attraction was that **it let him keep his sin and lose the shame.** If one parallel to modern syncretism is drawn, draw that one — it needs no stretching.

**New-age parallel — FAIR:** two-tier initiation (Elect/Hearers → teacher/seeker); gnosis marketed against "blind faith" (VI.v.7); embarrassment at flesh and a God born bodily (V.x.20); a cosmology that collapses on contact with evidence; the moral offload (V.x.18); syncretistic hospitality to Jesus as spirit while denying his flesh.

⚠️ **OVERREACH — do not write these:** (1) it was an organised missionary world religion with a founder, canon and hierarchy, not a vibe; (2) it was **severely ascetic**, not permissive — the Elect were celibate and vegetarian; (3) its dualism runs **opposite** to modern non-dual spirituality; (4) **he left for intellectual reasons, not moral disgust** — the astronomy failed and Faustus couldn't answer (V.iii–vii); staging his exit as revulsion invents a motive the text denies; (5) not a cult in the modern coercive sense.

### ⭐ Three Monica findings that could each carry a day

1. **Monica is named exactly ONCE in the whole _Confessions_** — the final paragraph of Book IX (IX.xiii.37), and only to ask readers to pray for her: "remember **Monnica** Thy handmaid." Thirteen books, one mention, at the end, as a request. (Manuscript spelling is _Monnica_.)
2. **Augustine does not present her as a saint.** She discouraged his marriage because she feared a wife would be "a clog and hindrance to my hopes" — his **career** (II.iii.8). And as a girl she had a secret drinking habit, broken when a servant called her a **"wine-bibber"** (IX.viii.18).
3. ⭐ **When he told her he had left the Manichees, she refused to celebrate.** VI.i.1: "she was **not overjoyed, as at something unexpected**… **Her heart then was shaken with no tumultuous exultation.**" **She would not take the half-answer as the answer.** For a series about a fire gone dim, that is a whole day on its own.
