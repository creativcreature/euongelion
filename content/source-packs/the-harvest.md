> 24 Jesus put before them another parable: "The kingdom of heaven is like a man who sowed good seed in his field. 25 But while everyone was asleep, his enemy came and sowed weeds among the wheat, and slipped away. 26 When the wheat sprouted and bore grain, then the weeds also appeared. 27 The owner's servants came to him and said, 'Sir, didn't you sow good seed in your field? ## 3. Lexicon — VERIFIED (BibleHub interlinear + Strong's + Sefaria, 2026-07-26)

### 3.1 The weeds and the sowing (Day 1)

- **ζιζάνιον** _zizanion_ **G2215** (always plural in the parable: ζιζάνια _zizania_) — darnel, "false grain" of uncertain (likely Semitic) origin, resembling wheat until the grain matures. **All 8 NT occurrences live in Matthew 13** (vv. 25, 26, 27, 29, 30, 36, 38, 40) — the word exists in Scripture only inside this parable.
- Sowing verbs: σπείρω _speirō_ **G4687** (vv. 24, 27, 37, 39); v. 25 uses the compound **ἐπέσπειρεν** _epespeiren_ from ἐπισπείρω _epispeirō_ — "sowed OVER, on top of" — an **NT hapax**. DRAFTING RULE: cite as "epispeirō (NAS 1986b; classic Strong's assigns it no number — the TR reads plain ἔσπειρεν; the over-sowing nuance rests on the critical text)." Never cite it as G1986.
- "While everyone was asleep" (v. 25): ἐν τῷ καθεύδειν τοὺς ἀνθρώπους — articular infinitive of καθεύδω _katheudō_ **G2518**.
- "Good seed": καλὸν σπέρμα _kalon sperma_ (G2570 + G4690) — v. 38 identifies it as "the sons of the kingdom."

### 3.2 The forbidden uprooting (Day 2)

- **ἄφετε** _aphete_ (v. 30) — aorist imperative of **ἀφίημι** _aphiēmi_ **G863**: send away, leave, permit, FORGIVE. The same verb in the Lord's Prayer — ἄφες ἡμῖν, "forgive us" (Matt 6:12). "Let both grow together" is grammatically a command built on Matthew's forgiveness verb. VERIFIED both on the G863 concordance page.
- **συναυξάνεσθαι** _synauxanesthai_ (v. 30) — συναυξάνω **G4885**, "to grow together" — **NT hapax**, this verse only.
- **συλλέγω** _syllegō_ **G4816** — the gathering verb used for BOTH gatherings: the weeds bundled for burning (vv. 28-30, 40) AND v. 41's "they will gather out of His kingdom every cause of sin" (συλλέξουσιν... τὰ σκάνδαλα).
- **σκάνδαλα** _skandala_ (v. 41) — σκάνδαλον **G4625**, literally the trap-stick of a snare; figuratively stumbling-blocks, causes of sin.

### 3.3 The harvest and the harvesters (Day 3)

- **θερισμός** _therismos_ **G2326** — harvest (vv. 30 ×2, 39: "the harvest is the completion of the age," συντέλεια αἰῶνος).
- **θεριστής** _theristēs_ **G2327** — reaper: **only 2 NT occurrences, both in this parable** (vv. 30, 39). v. 39 verbatim: οἱ δὲ θερισταὶ ἄγγελοί εἰσιν — "the harvesters are angels."
- **ὁ κόσμος** _ho kosmos_ **G2889** — v. 38 verbatim: ὁ δὲ ἀγρός ἐστιν ὁ κόσμος, "the field is the WORLD."
- v. 36: Τότε ἀφεὶς τοὺς ὄχλους ἦλθεν εἰς τὴν οἰκίαν — having left the crowds, He went into the house; the interpretation is private, indoors, at the disciples' request.

### 3.4 The shining (Days 3/5) — the Daniel connection

- **ἐκλάμψουσιν** _eklampsousin_ (v. 43) — ἐκλάμπω _eklampō_ **G1584**, "to shine OUT/forth" (Vincent: "a bursting into light... as the sun from behind a cloud") — **NT hapax**.
- **The allusion mechanism (state precisely):** **Theodotion's Greek Daniel 12:3 uses the exact same verb form — καὶ οἱ συνιέντες ἐκλάμψουσιν** ("and the wise will shine forth"). The Old Greek reads φανοῦσιν. So: the verbal link runs through Theodotion (the Daniel text the early church used); Matthew intensifies "like the brightness of the firmament / like the stars" to "like the SUN." DRAFTING RULE: call it an allusion/echo, never a quotation; commentators affirming it: Pulpit ("an undoubted reference to the substance of Daniel 12:3"), Cambridge, Ellicott, Meyer, Expositor's, Bengel.
- **Daniel 12:3 Hebrew:** וְהַמַּשְׂכִּלִים _vehammaskilim_ — Hiphil participle of שָׂכַל _sakal_ **H7919**, "those acting wisely"; יַזְהִרוּ _yazhiru_ — Hiphil imperfect of זָהַר _zahar_ **H2094**, "will shine"; כְּזֹהַר _kezohar_ — זֹהַר _zohar_ **H2096** "brightness" (only 2 OT occurrences: Ezek 8:2, Dan 12:3); וּמַצְדִּיקֵי הָרַבִּים _umatsdiqei harabbim_ — "those leading the many to righteousness": Hiphil participle of the VERB צָדַק _tsadaq_ **H6663** + הָרַבִּים **H7227**. DRAFTING RULE: H6666 (tsedaqah, the noun) belongs to Malachi's phrase, not Daniel's participle.

### 3.5 The prophetic frame (Day 5 + supports)

- **Malachi 4:2 (Heb 3:20):** שֶׁמֶשׁ צְדָקָה _shemesh tsedaqah_ — "sun of righteousness" (שֶׁמֶשׁ **H8121** construct + צְדָקָה **H6666**); "healing in its wings" — in-text form is **בִּכְנָפֶיהָ** _bikhnafeha_ (bet prefix + כָּנָף _kanaph_ **H3671** + 3fs suffix; print the prefixed form when quoting the text) + וּמַרְפֵּא _umarpe_ **H4832** (healing).
- **Joel 3:13 (Heb 4:13):** שִׁלְחוּ מַגָּל _shilchu maggal_ — imperative of שָׁלַח _shalach_ **H7971** + מַגָּל _maggal_ **H4038** "sickle" (**only 2 OT occurrences**: here and Jer 50:16); כִּי בָשַׁל קָצִיר _ki vashal qatsir_ — "for the harvest is ripe" (בָּשַׁל _bashal_ **H1310**, קָצִיר _qatsir_ **H7105**).

### 3.6 Rabbinic/botanical background (Day 1 cultural context)

- **Mishnah Kilayim 1:1 (verified via Sefaria):** הַחִטִּים וְהַזּוּנִין אֵינָן כִּלְאַיִם זֶה בָזֶה — "Wheat and zunin are not kilayim [forbidden mixed kinds] with one another." DRAFTING RULE: the Mishnah does NOT say "bastard wheat"; the underlying reasoning treats zunin as degenerate wheat. The wordplay lives in the **Jerusalem Talmud** (Kilayim 1:1): zunin named from the root of זנה — wheat "gone adulterous," degenerate. Phrase it: "the Mishnah rules wheat and darnel are not forbidden mixed kinds — because darnel was regarded as wheat gone wrong; the Yerushalmi's pun derives its name from the word for adultery."
- Botanically: _Lolium temulentum_, bearded darnel — grains hard to separate from wheat; flour toxic ("temulentum" = drunken). (Roman-law claim status pending §5 agent.)

### 5.6 Cultural-claims verdicts (drafting rules)

- **Darnel toxicity:** frame as HOST TO A TOXIC FUNGUS (modern sources: endophytic Neotyphodium producing loline alkaloids; older sources: ergot) — ISBE explicitly: not poisonous "unless infected with the mold ergot." Never say the seed itself is intrinsically poisonous. Easton (verbatim): "It bears the closest resemblance to wheat till the ear appears, and only then the difference is discovered."
- **Roman law:** Digest 9.2.27.14 (Ulpian citing Celsus): throwing darnel (lolium) or wild oats into another's crop to spoil it = an actionable CIVIL wrong (lex Aquilia; actio in factum + interdict quod vi aut clam). Say "Roman law gave the owner a lawsuit for malicious darnel-sowing" — NEVER "criminalized."

## 7. Christological / Canonical Connections (drafting guide)

- Jesus interprets this parable Himself (13:36–43) — one of two He decodes line by line; the week's authority question is settled by His own reading: field = world, harvest = end of the age, harvesters = angels.
- Matt 13:43 → Daniel 12:3: the righteous shining is resurrection hope, not general optimism (pending scholarly citations in §5).
- The sickle imagery runs Joel 3:13 → Mark 4:29 → Revelation 14:14–16: when it finally swings, it is in the hand of "One like the Son of Man" — judgment belongs to the Judge who bears scars.
- ἄφετε ("let both grow") is Matthew's forgiveness verb (pending lexical confirmation §3): the field's patience is cross-shaped — the Sower who forbids the servants' sickle will absorb evil's full harvest Himself before He ever swings His own.
- Matt 5:45 (sun on evil and good) is the Father's present posture; Mal 4:1–2 is its dated end: same sun, mercy now, sorting then.

## 8. Interpretive-Honesty Dossier (the head-on confrontation this parable requires)

- Jesus says "the field is the WORLD" (v.38) — not the church. Historical misuses run both directions: (a) reading it as forbidding all discipline/discernment (everything tolerated), (b) reading around it to justify purging people (inquisitions, purity crusades — the exact thing v.29 forbids). The week names both, keeps church discipline (Matt 18) distinct from eschatological uprooting, and lets Augustine's Donatist-era usage (pending §5) carry the historical weight.
- Darnel realism: indistinguishable from wheat until the ear forms — the parable assumes WE CANNOT RELIABLY TELL. Every application must keep this epistemic humility.
- The fire is in the text (v.42) — do not soften it; do not relish it. Judgment is real, dated, and God's; the tone is Jesus' own: warning plus the immediate pivot to the shining.
