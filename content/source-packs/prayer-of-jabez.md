## 3. Lexicon — VERIFIED (BibleHub interlinear + Strong's + morphology, 2026-07-12)

### 3.1 The name and the pain-word (Day 2)

- **יַעְבֵּץ** _Yabetz_ (yah-BETS), **H3258** — the man (1 Chr 4:9–10) AND a town (1 Chr 2:55). Strong's: "from an unused root probably meaning to grieve."
- **בְּעֹצֶב** _be'otsev_ — "in pain": preposition בְּ + noun **עֹצֶב** _otsev_, **H6090** (pain, sorrow, toil). H6090 occurs only 3× in the OT: 1 Chr 4:9, Psalm 139:24, Isaiah 14:3. By-form of עֶצֶב H6089.
- **The wordplay is metathesis** — the pain root is ע־צ־ב (ayin-tsade-bet); the name's consonants (minus the yod prefix) are ע־ב־ץ (ayin-bet-tsade): the last two consonants transposed. A regular formation "he causes pain" would be יַעְצֵב _ya'tsev_; the mother's naming is a sound-play. Scholars note the reversal of letters foreshadows the reversal of his circumstances (Pulpit Commentary; Kalimi, _Metathesis in the Hebrew Bible_). **DRAFTING RULE:** say "same three consonants, last two transposed" — never claim the name literally means "pain."

### 3.2 The prayer's four petitions (Days 3–6)

1. **אִם־בָּרֵךְ תְּבָרֲכֵנִי** _im-barekh tevarakheni_ — "Oh that you would bless me indeed." בָּרֵךְ = **Piel infinitive absolute**; תְּבָרֲכֵנִי = Piel imperfect 2ms + 1cs suffix; both root **בָּרַךְ** _barak_ **H1288**. Infinitive absolute + cognate finite verb = intensifying construction ("blessing you will bless me"). אִם opens a wish/oath formula.
2. **וְהִרְבִּיתָ אֶת־גְּבוּלִי** _vehirbita et-gevuli_ — "and enlarge my territory." וְהִרְבִּיתָ = **Hiphil** perfect 2ms of **רָבָה** _rabah_ **H7235** (make much/great); **גְּבוּל** _gevul_ **H1366** (border, boundary, territory).
3. **וְהָיְתָה יָדְךָ עִמִּי** _vehayta yadekha immi_ — "and that Your hand might be with me." **יָד** _yad_ **H3027**.
4. **וְעָשִׂיתָ מֵרָעָה** _ve'asita mera'ah_ — "and act [to keep me] from harm." **CRITICAL HONESTY NOTE:** the verb is **עָשָׂה** _asah_ **H6213** ("do, act, work") — there is NO Hebrew verb "keep" (shamar) in this clause; "keep me from harm" is translational smoothing. Literally: "and that You would work from harm." Noun **רָעָה** _ra'ah_ **H7451** (evil, harm, calamity). Cite as מֵרָעָה _mera'ah_.
5. **לְבִלְתִּי עָצְבִּי** _levilti otsbi_ — "that it may not pain me." לְבִלְתִּי H1115 ("so as not") + **Qal infinitive construct of עָצַב** _atsav_ **H6087** + 1cs suffix. Same **root** (ע־צ־ב) as the pain-noun in v.9, different Strong's entry (verb H6087 vs noun H6090). Keil-Delitzsch: "there is a play upon the name Jabez." Benson (verified via BibleHub commentaries on 4:10): "He uses this expression in allusion to his name." The prayer's last word circles back to his name. NOTE the WEB reads it "that I may not cause pain" — the form allows either the passive-experience or the causing reading; hold that ambiguity honestly in Day 6.

### 3.3 The address and the grant (Day 4)

- **וַיִּקְרָא יַעְבֵּץ לֵאלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל** _vayyiqra Yabetz le'lohei Yisrael_ — "And Jabez called TO the God of Israel" (prefixed lamed). קָרָא _qara_ **H7121** (call on — cf. Gen 4:26; 12:8); אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל _Elohei Yisrael_ (H430 construct + H3478).
- **וַיָּבֵא אֱלֹהִים אֵת אֲשֶׁר־שָׁאָל** _vayyave Elohim et asher-sha'al_ — "And God BROUGHT ABOUT what he asked." וַיָּבֵא = **Hiphil** consecutive imperfect of **בּוֹא** _bo_ **H935** (cause to come, bring); שָׁאָל = Qal perfect of **שָׁאַל** _sha'al_ **H7592** (ask, request). The literal "brought about/caused to come" is textually sound — God is the actor, not the formula.

## 9-A. Prosperity-Confrontation Dossier — VERIFIED (2026-07-12)

**Book facts:** Bruce Wilkinson, _The Prayer of Jabez: Breaking Through to the Blessed Life_ (Multnomah, Sisters, OR, 2000). 93 pages. ~8M copies in year one; 9M+ by 2002; 10M+ by 2008 (ECPA Diamond Award). ECPA Gold Medallion Book of the Year 2001. #1 New York Times bestseller; 94 weeks on the list; bestselling nonfiction book of 2001. Thesis: pray Jabez's prayer daily, word for word, with a 30-day challenge. (Preface line "a daring prayer that God always answers" circulates in two variants — quote it only via the 9Marks review attribution, not as a direct preface citation.)

**Published critiques (citable):**

- Mark R. Talbot, _Modern Reformation_ Nov/Dec 2001 (Vol. 10, No. 6): the book's "relentlessly upbeat stories, where praying Jabez's prayer has guaranteed triumph after triumph" don't acknowledge biblical suffering; the book "really says nothing about Christ and his cross." Talbot also credits that Wilkinson "wants to lead us to live lives that are more God-glorifying." (THE hinge quote for Day 3 → Day 4.)
- Greg Gilbert, 9Marks review (9marks.org, 2010): "What is unbiblical is that people are treating this prayer… as some kind of magic formula that will somehow hypnotize God into blessing us."
- Rodney Clapp & John Wright, "Misreading the prayer of Jabez: God as Santa," _The Christian Century_, Oct 23, 2002 (Vol. 119 No. 22, pp. 29–30): the book's vision is "markedly individualistic and insulating."
- John C. Beck Jr., _CTS Journal_ 8:1 (Winter 2002): "the 'cause' is wrongly attributed to the recitation of a prayer, not to the faithfulness found in the life of the one who believes God."
- Kenneth L. Woodward, "Platitudes or Prophecy?", _Newsweek_, Aug 27, 2001 — called it a "Christian mantra."
- Gary E. Gilley, _"I Just Wanted More Land" — Jabez_ (2001) — book-length critique; publisher imprint unverified, cite title/author/year only.

**Fair credit (verified — REQUIRED for charitable engagement):**

- Wilkinson's own late-chapter caveat (as quoted in Beck's CTS Journal review): "Of course, what you know about this or any other prayer won't get you anything.… It's only what you believe will happen and therefore do next that will release God's power for you and bring about a life change."
- His direct defense, verbatim from Baptist Press (James Dotson, Aug 9, 2001): "I've been preaching this for 30 years and nobody ever talked about money until it got to be a secular best-seller." And from his Jabez workbook (same article): the prayer is "not for my wishes, but His will. … It's not for what I want, but to accomplish what he wants."
- Christianity Today's July 2001 reader-response spread ran both directions; one defender: "If four million people are praying this prayer every day who might not otherwise pray at all, then it would seem absurd to protest."

## 6. Real Faith Stories — VERIFIED (provenance audit complete 2026-07-12; primary texts archived in session scratchpad and full-text searched)

### Day 2 — David Ring (b. Oct 28, 1953, Jonesboro, AR) — VERIFIED (self-reported primary testimony)

Born dead — eighteen minutes without oxygen — leaving cerebral palsy. Father died when he was 11; mother died of cancer when he was 14; orphaned, passed among relatives. Mocked for slurred speech; suicidal despair; converted in his late teens; began preaching 1973; five decades as full-time evangelist, speaking to 100,000+ people a year. Signature line from the platform: "I have Cerebral Palsy — what's your problem?"
**Sources:** David Ring, _Just As I Am: The Life of David Ring_ (1994); Ring, Wideman & Driver, _The Boy Born Dead_ (Baker Books, 2015); broadcast testimony "Victorious! The David Ring Story" (Dr. James Dobson Family Institute); davidring.org. **Caveat to carry:** details trace to Ring's own telling (allowed source type); no independent corroboration of the 18-minute figure, and no dispute of it either.

### Day 3 — George Müller (1805–1898) — VERIFIED from his own Narrative; famous breakfast story REJECTED as secondhand

**REJECTED:** the beloved tableau (children at empty table, baker knocks, milk cart breaks down) is NOT in Müller's writings — it comes from Abigail Townsend Luffe's much-later recollection (she was a child visitor, not his daughter-in-law). Do not use as Müller's record.
**USE INSTEAD (verbatim-verified, his own dated journal entry, Sept. 18, 1838):** After a week of daily prayer meetings with funds gone ("Sept. 12, still the trial continues"), the workers' last coins bought the day's food. Verified specifics from the journal entry itself (Gutenberg full text): "Brother T. had twenty-five shillings in hand, and I had three shillings"; the coins bought meat, bread, tea, and milk; the donor is "a lady from the neighborhood of London" who "took lodgings next door to the Boys' Orphan House." That afternoon a lady arrived with £3 2s 6d — she had been lodging FOUR OR FIVE DAYS NEXT DOOR to the Boys' Orphan House. Müller's own theology of the delay, verbatim: "That the money had been so near the Orphan Houses for several days without being given, is a plain proof that it was from the beginning in the heart of God to help us; but, because he delights in the prayers of his children, he had allowed us to pray so long; also to try our faith, and to make the answer much the sweeter."
**Source:** George Müller, _A Narrative of Some of the Lord's Dealings with George Müller_; American ed. _The Life of Trust_, ed. H. L. Wayland (1861), ch. X "Deliverance — 1838" (trial entries ch. IX). Full text: gutenberg.org/ebooks/27288 (verbatim verified).

### Day 4 — Monica of Thagaste (c. 331–387) — VERIFIED verbatim in two translations

**Exact reference: Augustine, _Confessions_, Book V, chapter 8, section 15.** Carthage, 383 AD: Monica followed Augustine to the harbor, terrified of Rome; he lied ("seeing off a friend"), persuaded her to spend the night at a memorial chapel to Cyprian, and sailed by night. "She on the morrow was there, frantic with sorrow."
**Augustine's wording (Pusey trans., verbatim):** "And what, O Lord, was she with so many tears asking of Thee, but that Thou wouldest not suffer me to sail? But Thou, in the depth of Thy counsels and hearing the main point of her desire, regardest not what she then asked, that Thou mightest make me what she ever asked."
**Pilkington (NPNF) rendering:** "granted not what she then asked, in order to make me what she was ever asking."
**Sources:** gutenberg.org/ebooks/3296 (Pusey, verbatim verified); newadvent.org/fathers/110105.htm (Pilkington, V.8 §15 confirmed).

He's got a bigger shovel"), itself not page-verified. NEVER put the line in LeTourneau's mouth in quotation marks; paraphrase the idea or attribute the reported customer remark as reported.

### Day 6 — John G. Paton (1824–1907) — "Jehovah's rain" VERIFIED verbatim; angel-guard story REJECTED as folklore

**REJECTED:** the oft-preached "chief asks about the hundreds of big men in shining garments with drawn swords" is NOT in the autobiography (both volumes full-text searched); it traces only to Billy Graham, _Angels_ (1975), uncited. Do not use.
**USE INSTEAD (verbatim-verified):** Kwamera, Tanna, late January 1862, the Mathiesons' station. ~10pm, armed warriors surround the mission house; church torched; fire walking the reed fence toward the house. Paton goes out alone, tears down the burning fence, is instantly surrounded: "Seven or eight savages had surrounded me, and raised their great clubs in air. I heard a shout — 'Kill him! kill him!'… but the Invisible One restrained them." Then a roaring wind from the south bends the flames away, followed by torrential rain: "'That is Jehovah's rain! Truly their Jehovah God is fighting for them and helping them. Let us away!' A panic seized upon them… and I was left alone, praising God for His marvellous works." Paton on that season's escapes: deliverance "neither by man nor of man's planning."
**Source:** _John G. Paton, Missionary to the New Hebrides: An Autobiography_, ed. James Paton (Hodder & Stoughton, 1889), ch. X "Farewell Scenes," pp. 346–348. Full text: archive.org/details/johngpatonmiss01patouoft (verbatim verified).

## 7. Historical / Cultural Context — VERIFIED (sources listed below, 2026-07-12)

- **Date & author:** Post-exilic, Persian period; anonymous ("the Chronicler"). Scholarly range: late 5th to mid-3rd century BC, most likely **4th century BC**. Phrase as: "written after the return from Babylonian exile, most likely in the 400s–300s BC." Since the 1960s–70s most scholars treat Chronicles and Ezra-Nehemiah as independent works.
- **Audience & purpose:** the restored Jerusalem/Yehud community. Chronicles legitimates the returned community as the continuation of pre-exilic Israel. The genealogies (1 Chr 1–9) condense vast history to highlight Levi (temple service) and Judah (Davidic monarchy) — identity, land continuity, worship legitimacy, Davidic hope.
- **The pericope:** vv. 9–10 sit inside Judah's genealogy (4:1–23) as an anecdote interrupting the list. Jabez appears with NO father or patronymic (Barnes: "introduced without description, or patronymic, as if a well-known personage"). These two verses are the entirety of his story — he appears in no other narrative. The Chronicler periodically breaks genealogies with short notices showing God answers those who seek him (cf. 1 Chr 4:39–43; 5:18–22). Enter the Bible (Luther Seminary): "His name is a curse that his prayer seeks to undo… The Chronicler seems to be saying that God responds to prayer."
- **The town Jabez (1 Chr 2:55):** "the clans of the scribes who lived at Jabez" — same spelling, same H3258; a town in Judah, site unknown (possibly near Kiriath-jearim per McClintock & Strong). The man-town connection is speculative (perhaps settled by his posterity) — present only as possibility. Rabbinic tradition (Targum to 1 Chr 2:55/4:9; b. Temurah 16a) identifies Jabez with Othniel and makes the scribes his Torah disciples — cite only as tradition, never as history.

**Context sources:** BibleHub interlinear/commentaries (1 Chr 4:9–10; 2:55); Enter the Bible (Luther Seminary), "1 Chronicles 4:9-10 — Jabez's Prayer"; Society for Old Testament Study Chronicles overview; CSB Apologetics Study Bible 1 Chronicles intro; Jewish Encyclopedia "Jabez"; McClintock & Strong "Jabez"; Kalimi, _Metathesis in the Hebrew Bible_.

## 8. Christological Connections (drafting guide)

- The prayer's grant is anchored in the covenant name — "the God of Israel." The covenant line runs Genesis 12 → 2 Samuel 7 → 2 Corinthians 1:20: every promise Yes in Christ.
- Jabez = "he will cause pain"; he prays to be free from pain and is granted it. Jesus = the Man of Sorrows (Isaiah 53) who prays "let this cup pass" (Matt 26:39) and is NOT granted it — so that the blessing could pass to us (Galatians 3:13–14). HONESTY NOTE: Isaiah 53's "sorrows" is מַכְאֹב (makob), NOT the עצב root of Jabez's name. The connection is thematic, not lexical — label it as an echo, never as a word-link.
- Enlarged borders find their fulfillment in the kingdom: the meek inherit the earth; the promise to Abraham becomes "all families of the earth."
