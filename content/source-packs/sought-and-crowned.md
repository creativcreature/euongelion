## 2. Lexicon + historical context — VERIFIED, with mandatory honesty notes

### THE FOUR FINDINGS THAT GOVERN HOW THIS SERIES IS WRITTEN

**1. "Lo-debar means no pasture" FAILS. So does "Lo-debar was a barren wasteland."**
The "no pasture" gloss requires repointing the vowels. And the wasteland reading is
contradicted by Scripture itself: **2 Samuel 17:28-29** has Machir son of Ammiel —
_the same Machir of Lo-debar who sheltered Mephibosheth_ — provisioning David's army
with beds, basins, wheat, barley, honey, curds and cheese. Machir is a wealthy
magnate. **Mephibosheth was not destitute in a desert; he was hidden in a rich man's
house.** Write the true version: a rival heir sheltered by a patron, out of sight,
across the Jordan. Spurgeon's "place of no pasture" (#1,836) may be QUOTED as
Spurgeon's reading; it may NOT be printed as the meaning of the name.

**2. THERE IS NO HEALING VERB ANYWHERE IN 2 SAMUEL 9.** The chapter ends
_"and he was lame in both his feet."_ If this series promises healing, the anchor
text withholds it. **Isaiah 35:6 uses the same lemma — pisseach — for the lame
leaping like a deer.** That is where the healing lives: eschatological, not in the
chapter. The series must not close the gap the text leaves open.

**3. "Roman adoption cancelled all debts" — REFUTED FROM PRIMARY SOURCES, not
merely unattested.** Origin: William Barclay, _Daily Study Bible_ on Rom 8:12-17,
citing nothing. Roman law explicitly closed that loophole:

- **Gaius 3.84** — liability extinguished by _capitis deminutio_, BUT "a praetorian
  action, however, based on a feigned rescission of their _capitis deminutio_, is
  granted to the creditors against them."
- **Gaius 4.38** — the praetor grants a fictitious action "rescinding or ignoring
  the defendant's _capitis deminutio_."
- **Digest 4.5.2.1** — the edict itself: creditors may sue "just as if such change
  of condition had not occurred." 4.5.2.2: those changed "remain naturally bound."
  4.5.2.3: "No one is exempt from the penalty for crime."
- **The category error that finishes it:** under ordinary _adoptio_ the adoptee was
  already _alieni iuris_ and could not own property or owe debts in his own name.
  There was nothing to cancel. The only form where he could have debts (_adrogatio_)
  is exactly the form Roman law refused to cancel them in.
  **DISCIPLINE:** do NOT write "scholars have refuted Barclay" — no such refutation is
  traceable. The claim is refuted **by the legal sources themselves**, which is stronger.

**4. THE TITLE IS SAFE, BUT DO NOT BLUR IT.** Psalm 103:4 _ham'aṭṭərēḵî_ is
genuinely **Piel** — "crowns" is a correct rendering. **But Mephibosheth is never
crowned.** The crowning belongs to the Psalm, not the narrative. The series may hold
them together thematically; it may not claim the chapter says something it doesn't.

### Further corrections — standard devotional claims that FAIL

- **"Mephibosheth couldn't be king because he was lame."** Jeremy Schipper's
  _Disability Studies and the Hebrew Bible: Figuring Mephibosheth in the David Story_
  (T&T Clark, 2006), ch. 3, uses ANE royal-representation evidence to DISPROVE this.
  It is the narrative's ideological construction, not ancient law.
- **"A lame man in that world begged."** Anachronism — a later source read back.
  _Encyclopaedia Judaica_: "there is hardly a reference to begging or to beggars,
  and there is, in fact, **no biblical Hebrew word for it**." Begging as an
  institution is rabbinic-period and NT (Mark 10:46; Acts 3:2). 2 Samuel 9 gives
  Mephibosheth land, labour (Ziba's fifteen sons, twenty slaves), a table and a
  household. **That is patronage, not destitution.**
- **"The lame were barred from the temple."** Far more contested than presented.
  _m. Kelim_ 1:6-9 maps ten degrees of holiness and the barrier at each —
  **disability appears nowhere**. Josephus _War_ 5.227 lists who was actually shut
  out: discharge and skin disease; blindness/lameness/deafness absent. The Temple
  Warning inscription is about **ethnicity**. **★ _m. Hagigah_ 1:1 is EXEMPTION, not
  exclusion** — "all are obligated… except for" women, children, the sick, the
  elderly, alongside the lame and blind, rationale stated: inability to ascend "on
  his own legs." Nobody claims women were barred. The Qumran texts are **sectarian
  and mostly eschatological** (1QSa = end-time council office; 1QM = apocalyptic war
  camp; 11QTemple = a temple never built), and 4QMMT B 49-54 gives an **epistemic**
  rationale, not disgust. **Do not overcorrect:** Olyan (CBQ 60.2, 1998) genuinely
  argues the cultic reading. Frame as "more contested than usually presented."
- **2 Samuel 5:8's "temple" is not in the Hebrew.** MT has bare הַבָּיִת, "the house."
  **LXX 2 Kingdoms 5:8** adds οἶκον Κυρίου, "house of the Lord." The temple reading
  is itself an ancient interpretive addition — and no temple existed in Jerusalem
  before Solomon. Revised JPS marks "meaning uncertain" twice in three verses;
  Josephus retells the episode (_Ant._ 7.61) with no v.8b at all; Chronicles omits it.
- **"Disability as punishment for sin."** **Exodus 4:11** attributes impairment
  directly to God with no mention of sin. And **John 9:2 is routinely quoted to
  establish a belief that John 9:3 refutes in the very next breath.**

### Adoption — four corrections

- **"_huiothesia_ is a technical Roman legal term Paul borrowed" — FALSE.** Deissmann,
  _Bible Studies_ (1901), 239: innumerable pre-Christian Aegean inscriptions use
  καθ᾽ υἱοθεσίαν; "Paul was availing himself of a generally intelligible figure."
  Ordinary Greek word, Greek institution. _Adoptio_ is the Latin. **Verified
  empirically absent from the LXX** — all 59 books grepped for θεσ; only ἀθεσία
  (81×) matches, with υἱός at 5,203 as sanity check. Also absent from Hatch & Redpath.
  **Safe to print as fact.**
- **"Adoption was irrevocable" — FALSE for the Greek world**, which is where most of
  Paul's readers lived. **Isaeus 6.44**: return to the original family is allowed
  "unless he leaves a legitimate son in the family which he quits." Gated, but legal.
  Roman adoptive sonship was revocable too (**D. 1.7.13**).
- **★ USE NERO, NOT THE ADOPTIVE EMPERORS.** Nerva–Trajan–Hadrian is **AD 96-180,
  after Paul**. The contemporary case is **Nero's adrogation by Claudius, AD 50** —
  five years before Romans. Tacitus _Ann._ 12.25-26: Claudius "gave Domitius, with
  his three years' seniority, precedence over his son"; Britannicus was "stripped
  little by little" and dead by February 55. **When Paul wrote _huiothesia_ to a
  congregation in the city of Rome, the man on the throne was an adopted son who had
  displaced the natural-born heir — and the heir was recently and notoriously dead.**
- **"Adoption was a familiar everyday experience" — OVERSTATED, and the correction
  improves the theology.** It was elite, property-driven, usually of adult kin;
  working people used fosterage. Kyu Seop Kim (_BTB_ 44, 2014): adopting _strangers_
  was rare, adopting _slaves_ legally distinct, adopting **in the presence of a
  legitimate heir** unusual. **Paul's metaphor is deliberately abnormal — and the
  abnormality is the point.**
- **Do NOT cite Burke as debunking the Roman reading** — he endorses it for Galatians.
  **Heim is Otago (2014), not Durham**, and does not say background is irrelevant;
  she rejects reading one legal system into Paul as though the metaphor were a legal
  transcript. Best summary (St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): "more recent
  scholarship is reaching a consensus that an either/or approach is insufficient."

### DAY 3 (PIVOT) — Floyd Collins, Sand Cave, Kentucky, February 1925

**The descent.** William Burke "Skeets" Miller, a reporter small enough to get down
the crawlway, went down to the trapped man **on seven different trips** — attested
from Miller's own **sworn testimony to the military court of inquiry**, via AP:
_"Miller said he found Collins in the cave on seven different trips."_
_Seward Daily Gateway_ (Alaska), 10 Feb 1925, p. 1 —
https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn87062169/1925-02-10/ed-1/?sp=1
**Print the number: seven.** He won the **1926 Pulitzer for Reporting** for it —
pulitzer.org, citation: _"For his work in connection with the story of the trapping
in Sand Cave, Kentucky, of Floyd Collins."_

**Collins's prayer — DOUBLE-SOURCED, three weeks apart, two archives:**

> "O Lord, dear Lord, gracious Lord, Jesus all powerful, get me out if it is thy
> will, but thy will be done."
> Miller's own copyrighted dispatch, syndicated — _Montgomery Advertiser_, 5 Feb 1925,
> p. 7; independently in _Literary Digest_, 28 Feb 1925.

**The line the series actually wants — it is not about rescue:**

> "tell everybody outside that I love every one of them and I am happy because so
> many are trying to help me… I'll keep helping all I can to move some of this rock."

**The refused harness.** Two separate events, do not merge them:

- **Mon 2 Feb** — Burdon's party pulls with rope and harness, moves him ~six inches.
  Miller had not yet arrived.
- **Tue 3 Feb evening** — a _second_ harness, **Homer Collins's attempt, and Floyd
  refused it**: _"Floyd begged his brother not to make such an effort for fear his
  leg might be pulled off."_
- The **jack came from the Courier-Journal**, worked by Burdon's party with Miller
  among them, same evening.
  **The trapped man called off his own brother's rescue because it would have cost him
  his leg.**

**Numbers — label which is which:** 5,000 standing at any moment; **20,000 visitors
across 8 February** by the L&N railroad's own estimate (_Yorkville Enquirer_,
10 Feb 1925, p. 4).

**The rock.** Collins called it "a huge rock"; his own estimate relayed by Gerald was
**50-60 lb**; the NPS says **27 lb** — a _post-mortem_ measurement taken only after
the rock was removed from his corpse. Honest framing: **a rock he could not name the
weight of, that the world later weighed.**

**Verified and usable:** a spontaneous religious service at the site — the crowd moved
to a hilltop northwest of the cave entrance and prayed for Collins (_Yorkville
Enquirer_, 10 Feb 1925). And grief became product within weeks: _"The Death of Floyd
Collins"_ was written by **Andrew Jenkins, a gospel songwriter licensed to preach at
twenty**, recorded by Vernon Dalhart (Edison Blue Amberol 5049, 1925). **Do not quote
the lyrics** — unverified — but the fact is verified and is a sermon in itself.

- Gregory of Nazianzus, _Oration_ I.V; Irenaeus, _Against Heresies_ V preface

### Day 4 — rehabilitation

- **ANCHOR — Athanasius, _De Incarnatione_ §14** — the spoiled portrait: the panel
  is not thrown away; the sitter returns so the likeness can be redone on the same
  wood. Rehabilitation rather than replacement.
- **Lewis, _Mere Christianity_ (1952), Book IV ch. 9 "Counting the Cost"** — the living house:
  "I find I must borrow yet another parable from George MacDonald. Imagine yourself
  as a living house. God comes in to rebuild that house. At first, perhaps, you can
  understand what He is doing. He is getting the drains right and stopping the leaks
  in the roof and so on: you knew that those jobs needed doing and so you are not
  surprised. But presently he starts knocking the house about in a way that hurts
  abominably and does not seem to make sense. What on earth is He up to? The
  explanation is that He is building quite a different house from the one you thought
  of—throwing out a new wing here, putting on an extra floor there, running up towers,
  making courtyards. You thought you were going to be made into a decent little
  cottage: but He is building a palace. He intends to come and live in it Himself."
  **Three details reproductions get wrong, all confirmed in two scans:** it opens by
  crediting MacDonald (dropping that line takes credit Lewis declined); "presently
  **he** starts" is lowercase; the clause takes an **em dash**, not a comma.
- Calvin on Psalm 103:3; Augustine, _Exposition of Psalm_ CIII.5

### Day 5 — crowning

- **ANCHOR — Augustine, _Exposition of Psalm_ CIII.6:** God crowns "His own gifts,
  not thy deservings." Exactly the series' claim about an unearned crown.
- **Lewis, _The Weight of Glory_ (preached 8 June 1941, St Mary the Virgin, Oxford;
  pub. _Theology_ Nov 1941; verified against the Macmillan New York 1949 first
  American edition):**
  - **THE CLOSING LINE FOR DAY 5:** "Meanwhile the cross comes before the crown and
    tomorrow is a Monday morning." Crowns the reader without letting them float off.
  - "we can be called in, welcomed, received, acknowledged… And to be at last
    summoned inside would be both glory and honour beyond all our merits and also
    the healing of that old ache." — Mephibosheth summoned to the table, in Lewis's words.
  - "There are no ordinary people. You have never talked to a mere mortal… immortal
    horrors or everlasting splendours."
- Spurgeon, _Treasury of David_ on Psalm 103:4; Athanasius §54; Irenaeus IV.20.7

### REJECTED — folklore and misattribution (do not use; do not re-research)

1. **NO SPURGEON SERMON ON 2 SAMUEL 9 EXISTS.** Zero across all 3,001 sermons. Any
   citation of "Spurgeon's Mephibosheth sermon" is fabricated. (#1,836 mentions
   Lo-debar; it is not a sermon on the chapter.)
2. **"The king's tablecloth covered his lame feet."** The most-quoted Mephibosheth
   line in circulation. **No attributable author. Folklore.** It is not Spurgeon.
   The series must not use it, and must not reach for the sentiment as if sourced.
3. "God loves each of us as if there were only one of us" — NOT in the _Confessions_.
4. "The glory of God is man fully alive" — a paraphrase, not a translation of Irenaeus.
5. "Prayer doesn't change God, it changes me" — **_Shadowlands_** (1993), screenplay
   by William Nicholson, spoken by Hopkins as a fictionalised Lewis. Nicholson also
   invented "We read to know that we are not alone."
6. "No pit so deep" — **Betsie** ten Boom's line, not Corrie's; popular form inserts
   "God's love."
7. "You don't have a soul. You are a soul." — apocryphal as Lewis AND not a quotation
   of MacDonald either. MacDonald's actual text, _Annals of a Quiet Neighbourhood_
   (1867) ch. XXVIII: "we talk as if we POSSESSED souls, instead of BEING souls."

### Flagged, not rejected

- Athanasius §54 translation variance
- _Miracles_ 1947-vs-modern edition variance (above)
- Owen's "fountain and cause" line — full clause reads "to his elect, and to them
  alone"; truncating misrepresents him. Set aside rather than trimmed.

### Bonus verified (usable anywhere)

- Lewis, _The Problem of Pain_ (1940) ch. 6: "God whispers to us in our pleasures,
  speaks in our conscience, but shouts in our pains: it is His megaphone to rouse a
  deaf world." **Keep the middle clause** — popular versions drop "speaks in our
  conscience" and destroy the three-term escalation.
