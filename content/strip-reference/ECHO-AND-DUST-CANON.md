# ECHO & DUST — canon (LOCKED, founder brief 2026-08-20)

The Daily Bread's daily strip. An adult philosophical comic disguised as a
simple vintage cartoon. Funny first, thoughtful immediately after,
occasionally devastating seconds later. Never lecture. Trust the reader.

## The trio

- **THEO — the question.** The human center; the reader who wandered in.
  Curious, earnest, optimistic nihilist; asks ordinary questions without
  noticing how enormous they are. Black male, rubber-hose design, white
  gloves, simple clothing, expressive eyes, strong silhouette. Age may shift
  15–50 (small afro → fuller afro → grey locs). NO chest symbol, NO flame.
- **ECHO — the constant.** Tallest. What stays true regardless of what we
  want: cause, consequence, inevitability, meaning. Lively, warm, playful,
  cheerfully comfortable with death/God/eternity — never a grim-reaper
  stereotype. Extremely simple rubber-hose silhouette, black hoodie with an
  ANATOMICAL HUMAN HEART on the chest, white gloves, long expressive limbs.
- **DUST — the variable.** Shortest. Chance, entropy, circumstance.
  Confident, dry, pessimistic, sometimes petty; usually the funniest line.
  Small round body with a cosmic/galaxy interior, tiny particles drifting
  off, rubber-hose limbs, white gloves, baseball-style cap. HIS HAT MAY
  CHANGE BETWEEN STRIPS (circumstance shifting) — never explained.

## Visual law (every generation, verbatim intent)

1920s rubber-hose / early black-and-white theatrical cartoon / vintage
newspaper comic. Black and white on warm off-white paper; heavy black ink;
film grain; slight print imperfections; bold silhouettes; minimal
backgrounds; strong facial acting; slightly imperfect hand-drawn linework.
FORBIDDEN: modern digital polish, glossy 3D, anime, Pixar-style rendering,
photorealism, color (beyond the warm paper tone).

## Format

3 horizontal panels (4 only when the joke truly needs the beat), thin black
gutters, minimal border, equal panels, readable on a phone. Rhythm:
P1 THE QUESTION (Theo notices something ordinary) → P2 THE ARGUMENT (Echo
interprets; Dust complicates or destroys) → P3 THE TURN (punchline /
reversal / sincerity / gut-punch / unanswered question — NEVER a summary).

## Writing law

3–15 words per balloon. No speeches, no therapy-speak, no motivational
poster language, no jargon, no fake profundity, no explaining symbolism, no
preaching. Faith, God, death, love, suffering, eternity may appear naturally
— reverence without losing humor. Small observations beat giant topics: the
enormous idea sneaks into the room. Surreal visual metaphor is welcome and
matter-of-fact to the characters.

## Production law (the automation contract)

1. WRITE FIRST: title, theme, central question, panel count, final turn,
   exact dialogue — the strip must work as writing before any art.
2. GENERATE as ONE wide image containing all panels (best intra-strip
   character consistency), balloons WITH the short dialogue in-model —
   v1 deviation from the brief's letter-separately rule, accepted because
   balloons are ≤15 words and every render is INSPECTED (typo = regenerate);
   move to programmatic lettering only if typos prove chronic.
3. ANCHORS MANDATORY: attach the founder-approved character sheet (and the
   most recent approved strip) as reference images on EVERY generation.
   Character consistency outranks visual novelty. Do not redesign.
4. The render lands as a `strip` DRAFT row → the founder's preview frames it
   NEEDS YOUR EYE → unrejected drafts print at the 7am flip (SA-114 rules).
5. Codex built-in image_gen ONLY (subscription). Nano Banana and Higgsfield
   are banned for this pipeline. Native size, no upscale hunting.

## North star

One human question. One laugh. One idea worth carrying the rest of the day.
The best strips make the reader unsure which one hurt them.
