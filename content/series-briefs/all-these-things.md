## One-line theme

God is steady, His promise is concrete, and we go on being frightened anyway —
so this week is about what faith actually does in the gap between the two.

## The spine, decoded into structure

The founder's sentence is already a week. Each clause is a day:

| Founder's clause                                              | Day           |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------- |
| "Seek ye First the Kingdom"                                   | 1             |
| "We continue to become anxious inspite of His steadyness"     | 2             |
| "standing deeply on Faith (the movie silence)"                | 3 — the pivot |
| "Him Being a Rock with no need for mediation in relationship" | 4             |
| "He is the wholeness, and all things come from Him"           | 5             |

Two of his phrases turn out to be **in the text verbatim**, and the week is built
on that:

- **"all things come from Him"** — 1 Chronicles 29:14, KJV: _"for all things come
  of thee, and of thine own have we given thee."_ Day 5.
- **"He is the wholeness"** — this is _shalom_. Greek _eirēnē_ in Philippians 4:7
  carries the Hebrew freight: not the absence of trouble, but wholeness. Day 5's
  vocab module.
- **"His steadyness"** — Hebrew _emunah_ (H530), the word under "the righteous
  will live by faith" (Habakkuk 2:4). It means firmness/steadiness before it
  means belief. Day 3's vocab module, and the answer to "how to faith."

## 7-day chiastic outline (Monday start — SA-123)

### Day 1 (Mon, A) — Hook: "Seek Ye First"

- Anchor: **Matthew 6:33 — KJV**
- Theme: The command and the promise, in that order. What "first" means when it
  is a rank and not a step in a sequence — and what "all these things" actually
  refers to (food, drink, clothing; the passage defines its own phrase, and it is
  not a wealth list).
- **Cross-testament (SA-032):** NT anchor → OT in 1 Kings 3:5-13. Solomon asks
  for an understanding heart first and is given "that which thou hast not asked,
  both riches, and honour." The passage supplies its own Old Testament case
  study — and then Matthew 6:29 turns and says even that glory was not arrayed
  like one lily. Seek-first, added-to, and then relativised, inside one chapter.
- Meta-story line: KINGDOM.
- PaRDeS: peshat. Interactive: breath.

### Day 2 (Tue, B) — Build: the word that means "pulled apart"

- Anchor: **Matthew 6:25-27 — ASV** (literal "be not anxious", pairs with vocab)
  with **Philippians 4:6 — KJV** ("Be careful for nothing") as the secondary.
- Theme: _merimnaō_. Name once, early: these verses get used as a rebuke to
  people who are already suffering, and that use cannot survive the word itself —
  the same verb is used **approvingly** of Timothy in Philippians 2:20 and of the
  church's members in 1 Corinthians 12:25. A word Paul praises in one letter is
  not a word he criminalises in another. Then teach the text.
- The translation split is doing real teaching here: KJV's "add one cubit unto
  his stature" against BSB's "add a single hour to his life" (_hēlikia_, which
  carries both height and lifespan). Show the reader the seam rather than hiding
  it.
- **Cross-testament:** NT anchor → OT in Exodus 16. Manna is gathered daily; what
  is hoarded breeds worms. "Give us this day our daily bread" (Matt 6:11) is the
  same economy, four verses earlier in the same sermon.
- PaRDeS: remez. Interactive: journal.

### Day 3 (Wed, C) — **PIVOT**: When He Says Nothing

- Anchor: **Psalm 22:1-2 — BSB** (OT-anchored day)
- Theme: The honest case. What if the promise does not visibly arrive? This is
  where the week either earns the reader's trust or loses it. Habakkuk gets the
  answer that is not an answer — "though it lingers, wait for it" — and then the
  line the whole New Testament will quote: "the righteous will live by faith,"
  _emunah_, steadiness. Faith here is not a feeling produced on demand; it is
  remaining in place.
- **The "aha":** the silence is not evidence of absence, and the proof is that
  God the Son went through it. Jesus quoted Psalm 22:1 from the cross — the
  cry of abandonment is _inside_ the Godhead, in the canon, on the record.
- **Cross-testament:** OT anchor → NT in Matthew 27:45-46. Also the _Silence_
  (Endō Shūsaku, 1966) material — the founder's own reference — pending the
  quotes agent's verbatim verification of a named translation.
- PaRDeS: derash. Interactive: physical.

### Day 4 (Thu, B′) — Apply: The Rock You Do Not Need An Appointment To Reach

- Anchor: **Philippians 4:5 — KJV** ("The Lord is at hand") with **Deuteronomy
  32:4** and **Psalm 62:1-2**.
- Theme: Two things at once, and they belong together. He is _tsur_ — rock, the
  immovable thing — and He is _engys_, near. Immovability usually implies
  distance; here it does not. And there is no intermediary to get past: the veil
  tore top to bottom, and the throne is approached "with confidence."
- **Cross-testament:** OT (Deut 32:4 / Ps 62) → NT (Matt 27:51; Heb 4:14-16;
  1 Tim 2:5).
- PaRDeS: derash. Interactive: memory.

### Day 5 (Fri, A′) — Resolve: All Things Come Of Thee

- Anchor: **Philippians 4:7 — KJV** ("the peace of God, which passeth all
  understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds") with **Matthew 6:32-34**.
- Theme: _phroureō_ is a garrison word — a military detail posted around a city.
  The peace is not a mood; it is a guard placed. And _eirēnē_ carries _shalom_:
  wholeness. The week lands on 1 Chronicles 29:14 — "all things come of thee" —
  which is where the founder's sentence and the text meet.
- **Cross-testament:** NT anchor → OT in 1 Chronicles 29:11-14.
- **Christological link (spec §9.4, required by Day 5):** Colossians 1:17 — "in
  Him all things hold together." The wholeness has a name.
- PaRDeS: sod. Interactive: art.

### Day 6 (Sat) — Recap

- The five takeaways integrated; no new teaching. Carries the required
  Further-Your-Learning set: verified videos, free primary texts, a passage
  trail, a guided read. Opens with two-minute-open-v2 like the teaching days.

### Day 7 (Sun) — Sabbath

- Anchor: **Matthew 6:34 — KJV** ("Sufficient unto the day is the evil thereof")
  with Lamentations 3:26 ("It is good to wait quietly").
- One-line invitation: stop gathering. The week ends where the passage ends —
  with one day, which is all anyone was ever given.

## Translation rationale (per anchor)

| Day | Reference          | Translation | Why this rendering for this passage                                                                                                                   |
| --- | ------------------ | ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Matthew 6:33       | KJV         | "Seek ye first" is the phrasing the passage is carried in and the founder's own; imperative weight and liturgical memory.                             |
| 2   | Matthew 6:25-27    | ASV         | Renders _merimnaō_ as "be not anxious" — literal, and sits directly beside the vocab module. KJV's "take no thought" would fight the teaching.        |
| 2   | Philippians 4:6    | KJV         | "Be careful for nothing" preserves the older sense of _care_ = burden, which is exactly the point being made.                                         |
| 3   | Psalm 22:1-2       | BSB         | Clarity over cadence on the day the reader may be in actual distress; "so far from my words of groaning" is plainer than KJV's "words of my roaring." |
| 3   | Habakkuk 2:3-4     | BSB         | Modern clarity for the "though it lingers, wait for it" promise.                                                                                      |
| 4   | Philippians 4:5    | KJV         | "The Lord is at hand" holds the spatial/temporal ambiguity the day is built on; BSB's "is near" collapses it.                                         |
| 4   | Deuteronomy 32:4   | BSB         | "He is the Rock, His work is perfect" — clean declarative for the vocab pairing with _tsur_.                                                          |
| 5   | Philippians 4:7    | KJV         | "passeth all understanding" and "shall keep" — _keep_ is the garrison verb, and KJV's cadence is the one readers carry.                               |
| 5   | 1 Chronicles 29:14 | KJV         | "for all things come of thee" is the founder's phrase verbatim; no modern rendering improves it.                                                      |
| 7   | Matthew 6:34       | KJV         | "Sufficient unto the day is the evil thereof" — the sabbath day is a cadence day.                                                                     |
