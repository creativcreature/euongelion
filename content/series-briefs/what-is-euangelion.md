# Series Brief: What is Euangelion?

**Slug:** `what-is-euangelion`
**Track:** Doctrine / Foundations
**Pathway target:** sleep
**Anchor passage:** Mark 1:1, Mark 1:14–15
**Anchor translation:** BSB
**Meta-story placement:** INCARNATION → MINISTRY → CHURCH (the gospel announcement spanning the whole arc)
**Length:** 7 days (5 chiastic + recap + sabbath)
**Estimated total words:** ~22,500 across teaching modules; ~2,500 in scripture/vocab/auxiliary modules

---

## One-line theme

_The good news — what the word actually meant before church language buried it, what it meant when Jesus started saying it, and why it still matters._

---

## Why this is the pilot

Pilot for the Sleep pathway: the drifted, the skeptical, the never-was. This series:

- Defines a foundational term every visitor encounters on the homepage (the site is named "Euangelion")
- Assumes zero biblical literacy — no insider language, no liturgical assumptions
- Treats the reader as an adult: explains the historical world before quoting Scripture as authority
- Carries the weight of a foundational claim without demanding agreement on Day 1
- Lands in invitation, not prescription

It is also the natural first onboarding series for any new user who finishes the Soul Audit unsure where to start.

---

## Voice register (Sleep pathway)

- Define every theological term the first time it appears — _grace_, _gospel_, _atonement_, _kerygma_, _propitiation_. Glossing in-line, not parenthetically.
- Acknowledge the reader's possible posture: skepticism, fatigue, prior religious harm.
- Avoid altar-call language. The series ends in _invitation_, not _decision_.
- Quote pre-modern voices the reader does not need to already trust (Augustine, Spurgeon) before quoting modern Christian voices (Lewis, Bonhoeffer, Wright). The historical distance carries weight a contemporary author cannot.
- Direct second-person ("you") only in A and A' modules and `reflection` modules. Teaching B/C/B' uses "we" or third person.

## 7-day chiastic outline

### Day 1 (A) — Hook: "A Word You Already Know in a Language You Don't"

- **Anchor:** Mark 1:1 (BSB) — _"The beginning of the gospel of Jesus Christ, the Son of God."_
- **Theme:** _Euangelion_ (Greek, εὐαγγέλιον) is not a religious word. It was a public-square word — a herald's announcement of something that had already happened: a battle won, a king enthroned, a son born to a Caesar. Mark borrows the word on purpose. He's making a political claim before he makes a theological one.
- **Meta-story line:** _Today we are at the moment the announcement begins. The Old Testament has been waiting for this word. The New Testament opens with it._
- **Vocab module:** εὐαγγέλιον (_euangelion_) — etymology (eu- "good" + angelos "messenger / message"); ancient usage; Strong's G2098.
- **Interactive:** Breath prayer ("good news / has come") — invitation to slow the reading down.
- **Christological note:** Mark is not announcing a teaching. He is announcing a person.

### Day 2 (B) — Build: "The Word Before the Word"

- **Anchor:** Isaiah 52:7 (KJV) — _"How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of him that bringeth good tidings, that publisheth peace; that bringeth good tidings of good, that publisheth salvation; that saith unto Zion, Thy God reigneth!"_
- **Translation rationale:** KJV chosen for cadence — Isaiah's prophetic Hebrew sings in a way modern translations flatten. The anchor passage is heard as music before it is parsed as content.
- **Theme:** _Euangelion_ doesn't begin with the New Testament. The Greek word translates a Hebrew concept — _besorah_ — that runs through Isaiah. The gospel was already a word before it was an announcement.
- **Vocab module (secondary):** בְּשׂוֹרָה (_besorah_) — the Hebrew counterpart, from a verb meaning "to bring tidings" (Strong's H1309).
- **Bridge module:** Isaiah's exiles waiting for a watchman's cry → the Roman world waiting for an emperor's herald → the modern reader waiting for something they can't quite name.
- **Interactive:** Journaling prompt — _"What good news are you waiting to hear?"_

### Day 3 (C) — Pivot: "The Messenger and the Message Are the Same Person"

- **Anchor:** 1 Corinthians 15:1–8 (BSB) — Paul's earliest formal summary of the gospel.
- **Translation rationale:** BSB chosen for clarity in Pauline argument — the compressed creedal structure ("died … buried … raised … appeared") needs to land cleanly.
- **Theme (the "aha"):** Other religions and ideologies have a teacher who delivers a message. Christianity is structurally different. The Messenger and the Message are the same person. The good news is not a doctrine Jesus taught — _Jesus is the good news._ Paul says "the gospel I preached to you" — and then describes a person, not a system.
- **Reading level:** This is the C module — the most theologically dense day. Permission to use _kerygma_, _creedal formula_, _sub-Pauline tradition_ — defined in line.
- **External voice (1 of 2):** Athanasius, _On the Incarnation_ (4th century) — on why God had to come in person.
- **External voice (2 of 2):** Charles Spurgeon, "Christ Crucified" (1858 sermon) — on the singularity of the gospel as a _person_.
- **Interactive:** Physical practice — open palms, then closed hands — the gesture of receiving rather than producing.
- **Christological resolution:** This is the explicit pivot of the entire series.

### Day 4 (B-prime) — Apply: "What Receiving It Looks Like"

- **Anchor:** Romans 1:16–17 (BSB) — _"For I am not ashamed of the gospel, because it is the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes…"_
- **Theme:** Receiving the gospel is not assenting to a list of beliefs — it is trust. Specifically: trust that the work has already been done. The Reformation word is _sola fide_ (faith alone) — not as a slogan but as a description of the posture: empty hands, not full ones.
- **Story module:** A modern narrative — someone who tried for years to "be a good Christian" and discovered that effort was the obstacle, not the doorway.
- **Bridge module:** Roman _pistis_ (allegiance, trust) → Hebrew _emunah_ (faithful response) → modern English "faith" (often heard as private belief). Restoring the muscle of the original word.
- **External voice:** Martin Luther, _Preface to Romans_ (1522) — on faith as a "living, busy, active, mighty thing."
- **Interactive:** Scripture memory — Romans 1:16, with a memorization technique (chunking by phrase).

### Day 5 (A-prime) — Resolve: "An Invitation, Not a Conclusion"

- **Anchor:** Acts 8:26–39 (BSB) — Philip and the Ethiopian eunuch.
- **Theme:** The series doesn't end in a sinner's prayer. It ends in the way the Bible itself ends most gospel announcements — with a question and an open road. _"What prevents me from being baptized?"_ The eunuch is on a journey home, reading Isaiah, and the Spirit puts a stranger in his path. The reader is on a journey too.
- **Reading level:** Returns to A-level (8th grade). Intentionally simple. The chiasm closes by echoing Day 1's accessibility.
- **Mirrors Day 1:** opens with _euangelion_, closes with the gospel received — same word, transformed register.
- **Interactive:** Art response — find or recall an image that names what is happening inside.
- **Closing prayer:** Permissive, not coercive. "If You're real, this is where I am" rather than "I now confess."

### Day 6 — Recap: "Five Things Worth Remembering"

- **No new teaching.**
- Five short sections, one per teaching day, surfacing the key insight from each.
- Closes with a single integration question: _"Which of these landed hardest? Sit with that one."_
- Transitions to Day 7's silence.

### Day 7 — Sabbath: "Be Still"

- **Anchor:** 2 Corinthians 5:17–21 (BSB) — _"…God was reconciling the world to Himself in Christ … Therefore we are ambassadors for Christ, as though God were making His appeal through us. We implore you on behalf of Christ: Be reconciled to God."_
- **Anchor translation rationale:** BSB carries the Pauline argument cleanly; the Sabbath day's posture is reception, not analysis, so a single clear modern rendering serves better than a poetic one.
- **Invitation:** _Close the app. Sit with this for the day. We'll be here tomorrow._
- **Prayer (one line):** "Father, I have nothing to do today but be with You. Amen."

All sources are public domain unless noted.

- **Augustine**, _Confessions_ Book 7 (~400 AD) — Day 5, on the moment of conversion
- **Charles Spurgeon**, "Christ Crucified" sermon (1858) — Day 3, on the singularity of the message
- **Martin Luther**, _Preface to Romans_ (1522) — Day 4, on living faith
- **Matthew Henry**, _Commentary on the Whole Bible_ (1708–1710) — Days 1, 2, 4 — verse exposition
- **John Calvin**, _Commentaries_ (1540s–1550s) — Day 3, on 1 Cor 15
- **John Chrysostom**, _Homilies on Romans_ (~390 AD) — Day 4, on Romans 1:16
- **Eusebius**, _Ecclesiastical History_ Book 3 (~325 AD) — Day 1, on Mark and his sources

- **N.T. Wright** — Day 1, on _euangelion_ in Roman imperial context (citation: _How God Became King_, 2012)
- **Tim Keller** — Day 4, on faith as trust (citation: _King's Cross_, 2011)
