## Origin (recorded per devo-go Phase 1)

This reading was commissioned in a conversation on 13 August 2026. A colleague asked
for something on **healing, being patient**, and the founder added **rest**. What the
founder said in the five minutes that followed is the spine of this reading, and every
one of its moves is present in that conversation:

1. The wilderness is usually where God is teaching, not punishing.
2. "I need to rest more and give myself grace" — and her answer, "Yes, grace. God's grace."
3. The Martha problem — trying to fix everything and serve everything when what God wants
   is presence; and how being distracted by life is often what gets a person taken
   somewhere they have to focus.
4. **"Pay attention to what you pay attention to."** Whatever your mind is on, it is on it
   for a reason — find out why, and take the why to God.
5. Running the race: a marathon, endurance, patience, and cutting out distractions.
6. The image the whole reading is named for: _when you look at the sun and then try to
   look at anything else, all you can see is the sun._

The reading is written so it stands on its own for any reader. Nothing personal to the
colleague appears in it.

renewed in the waiting, and the waiting is governed by what the eyes are fixed on.

## The single day

### Day 1 (A) — The One Thing You Can Still See

- **Two-Minute Open anchor:** Isaiah 40:28-31 (KJV) — the promise of renewed strength,
  spoken to exiles, and given to those who _wait_.
- **Deep-dive anchor:** Hebrews 12:1-2 (KJV) — run with patience, looking unto Jesus.
- **Meta-story line:** WILDERNESS — the stretch where God's people are being taught by
  what has been taken away from them.
- **The "aha":** The Greek of Hebrews 12:1-2 puts three words in a row that all describe
  the same spatial problem. The weight (ὄγκος), the sin that _stands all around_
  (εὐπερίστατος), and the answer — ἀφορῶντες, _looking away from everything else at one
  thing_. Martha's word in Luke 10:40 (περισπάω, _dragged all around_) is the same
  geometry from the other side. The instruction is never "care about less." It is "look
  at one thing, and the rest loses its grip by consequence."
- **Where it lands:** Isaiah 40:31 puts the running AFTER the waiting. Elijah under the
  broom tree gets bread and sleep twice before God says a word to him. Rest is not the
  reward at the end of the race; it is the thing the race is run on.

## Translation rationale (per anchor)

| Module | Reference       | Translation | Why this rendering for this passage                                                                                                                                 |
| ------ | --------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Open   | Isaiah 40:28-31 | KJV         | The cadence of "they shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint" is the passage's memory hook; BSB flattens it.                                |
| Deep   | Hebrews 12:1-2  | KJV         | KJV renders ὑπομονή as **patience** — the exact word asked for — and "Looking unto Jesus" carries the directional force of ἀφορῶντες better than "fix our eyes on." |
| Deep   | Luke 10:41-42   | KJV         | "Careful and troubled" and "one thing is needful" are the renderings this passage is remembered in, and "needful" is stronger than "necessary."                     |
| Deep   | 1 Kings 19:4-8  | BSB         | Narrative prose; BSB's plainness keeps the scene's bluntness ("I have had enough, LORD").                                                                           |
| Deep   | Deuteronomy 8:2 | BSB         | A long conditional sentence; BSB keeps it readable.                                                                                                                 |
| Deep   | Hosea 2:14      | BSB         | "I will allure her and lead her to the wilderness, and speak to her tenderly" — BSB is the warmest accurate rendering here.                                         |

## External voices drawn from

- **John Milton**, _Sonnet 19_ ("When I consider how my light is spent") and _Second
  Defence of the English People_ (1654) — a man whose ability to work was taken from him,
  arguing himself out of the Martha complaint in fourteen lines. Story module.
- **Blaise Pascal**, _Pensées_, Lafuma 136 / Brunschvicg 139 — on why a person cannot stay
  quietly in a room, with the caveat that the famous English version is a paraphrase.
  Insight module.
