# Series Brief: Looking at the Sun

**Slug:** looking-at-the-sun
**Track:** Disciplines
**Pathway target:** awake
**Anchor passage:** Hebrews 12:1-2
**Anchor translation:** KJV
**Meta-story placement:** WILDERNESS
**Length:** 7 days, Monday start

## Origin (recorded per devo-go Phase 1)

Commissioned in conversation on 13 August 2026. A colleague asked for something on
**healing, being patient**; the founder added **rest**, and then spoke a
five-minute reading on the spot. What he said is the spine, and every move in the
week traces to it:

1. The wilderness is usually where God is teaching, not punishing.
2. "I need to rest more and give myself grace" — her answer: "Yes, grace. God's grace."
3. The Martha problem — trying to fix and serve everything when what God wants is
   presence; and how being distracted is often what gets a person taken somewhere
   they have to focus.
4. **"Pay attention to what you pay attention to."** Whatever your mind is on, it
   is on it for a reason — find out why, and take the why to God.
5. Running the race: a marathon, endurance, patience, cutting out distractions.
6. The image the series is named for: _when you look at the sun and then try to
   look at anything else, all you can see is the sun._

Nothing personal to the colleague appears in the text. It reads for a stranger.

### Correction recorded (2026-08-14)

This shipped first as a **single reading**. That was the assistant's call, made

says to lock the week shape before writing anything. The founder corrected it the

## One-line theme

Endurance is not a matter of strength but of attention: the strength for the race
is renewed in the waiting, and the waiting is governed by what the eyes are fixed
on.

## 7-day chiastic outline (Monday start — sabbath lands on Sunday)

### Day 1 (A) — Mon — Hook: _The Promise Was Not Made to the Strong_

- Anchor: Isaiah 40:28-31 — KJV
- Theme: Renewal is promised to people already spent, and the running comes
  _after_ the waiting. Even youths fall; that is the verse's own contrast.
- Meta-story line: WILDERNESS — spoken to exiles whose forced labour is over.
- Vocab: קָוָה _qavah_ (H6960)
- Story: Amy Carmichael, writing _Rose from Brier_ from the bed she never left.
- Video: BibleProject, Isaiah 40-66 (`_TzdEPuqgQg`)

### Day 2 (B) — Tue — Build: _The Weight and the Thing That Stands Around_

- Anchor: Hebrews 12:1 — KJV
- Theme: Two separate problems — weight (ὄγκος, no moral charge) and the sin that
  stands all around (εὐπερίστατος, a hapax). And the cloud of witnesses turns out
  to be people who ran the whole distance without ever seeing the end.
- Vocab: εὐπερίστατος _euperistatos_ (G2139), with ὄγκος and ὑπομονή
- Story: Adoniram Judson — six years to one convert, seventeen months in irons.
- Video: BibleProject, Hebrews (`1fNWTZZwgbs`)

### Day 3 (C) — Wed — Pivot: _Bread, and Then Sleep, and Then a Word_

- Anchor: 1 Kings 19:3-9 — BSB
- Theme: God's first answer to a man asking to die was food; His second was more
  food; the word came last, and quietly. Rest is provisioning, not reward.
- The "aha": the wilderness is not the interruption of the assignment. It is
  where the assignment is supplied.
- Vocab: דְּמָמָה _demamah_ (H1827) — the thin silence
- Story: Spurgeon on his own fainting fits, prescribing sea air rather than grace.
- Video: BibleProject, 1-2 Kings (`bVFW3wbi9pk`)

### Day 4 (B-prime) — Thu — Apply: _Martha, Martha_

- Anchor: Luke 10:38-42 — KJV
- Theme: περισπάω is passive — it was being done to her. The correction names the
  _number_, not the work. And the good portion is the one thing in that room that
  cannot be taken away.
- Vocab: περισπάω _perispaō_ (G4049), with μερίς _meris_ (G3310)
- Story: Brother Lawrence in the noise and clutter of his kitchen.

### Day 5 (A-prime) — Fri — Resolve: _Looking at the Sun_

- Anchor: Hebrews 12:2 — KJV
- Theme: ἀφοράω is a turning-away that is a turning-toward, in one motion, held
  continuously. Nothing in anyone's circumstances gets edited; what changes is
  what everything else is seen through. And the One being looked at is seated.
- Vocab: ἀφοράω _aphoraō_ (G872)
- Story: Milton, blind, arguing himself out of the day-labour complaint.

### Day 6 — Sat — Recap

- The five takeaways integrated, no new teaching, plus a Further-Your-Learning
  section: the verified videos, free primary texts, a passage trail, a guided read.

### Day 7 — Sun — Sabbath

- Anchor: Psalm 23:1-3 (KJV) — "He maketh me to lie down."
- One invitation, then silence. ~400 words.

## Translation rationale (per anchor)

| Day | Reference       | Translation | Why this rendering                                                                                                                                   |
| --- | --------------- | ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Isaiah 40:28-31 | KJV         | "They shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint" is the cadence the passage is remembered in; BSB flattens it.                 |
| 2   | Hebrews 12:1    | KJV         | Renders ὑπομονή as **patience** — the word she asked for — where modern versions print _endurance_. Same Greek word; the week turns on that.         |
| 3   | 1 Kings 19:3-9  | BSB         | Narrative prose. BSB keeps the bluntness of "I have had enough, LORD."                                                                               |
| 3   | 1 Kings 19:12   | KJV         | "A still small voice" is the rendering the passage lives in; the vocab module handles what the Hebrew actually says.                                 |
| 4   | Luke 10:38-42   | KJV         | "Careful and troubled" and "one thing is needful" — "needful" is stronger than "necessary," and "careful" in its older sense is the whole diagnosis. |
| 5   | Hebrews 12:2    | KJV         | "Looking unto Jesus" carries the directional force of ἀφορῶντες better than "fix our eyes on."                                                       |
| 7   | Psalm 23:1-3    | KJV         | Sabbath wants cadence over precision.                                                                                                                |
