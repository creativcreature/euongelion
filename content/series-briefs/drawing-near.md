## One-line theme

Nearness was always the point, it was never freely available, and then the veil tore — so the question stops being _may I come_ and becomes _how do I carry a freedom this expensive_.

## The two nested frames

- **Outer:** obedience → relationship.
- **Inner:** permission → proximity.

Every day points at one of them. The weight of the week sits on the **invitation**, not the warning. Day 5's responsibility is the _third consequence of nearness_, not a separate rebuke.

## Soul Audit keywords

distance, unworthiness, access, shame, hiding, freedom, belonging, adoption, reverence, welcome

## The rhyme the week is built on

Genesis 3:24 — cherubim and a flaming sword "to keep **the way** of the tree of life."
Romans 14:13 — "that no man put a stumblingblock or an occasion to fall in his brother's **way**."

God obstructs a road to life once, and Genesis 3:22 states the reason: "lest he put forth his hand, and take also of the tree of life, and eat, and **live for ever**." He bars the tree so a broken man is not sealed broken forever. Paul then names the one road you are still capable of blocking. Day 1 and Day 5 are that sentence pointed two directions.

Genesis 3:8 — they hid from the presence _before_ they were driven out. The distance was chosen before it was enforced.

## Editorial stance (founder, from the thematic)

- **Leviticus discipline:** vocabulary and logic of approach only. No sacrificial taxonomy, no Day-of-Atonement walk-through. 17:11 earns its place by explaining _why blood_.
- **Baggage, named once and early, then teach the text:** (1) the exile as pure punishment — Genesis 3:22 gives the stated reason; (2) "an angry Father extracting payment from a kind Son" — Leviticus 17:11 says God _gave_ the blood, Hebrews 10:20 says the veil was His own flesh; (3) "freedom means it does not matter what I do" — 1 Corinthians 10:23, once.
- **AI tells (SA-130) apply in full.** This series _quotes_ triads ("Holy, holy, holy") and antithetical parallelism ("not to destroy, but to fulfil"). Quoted they are content; imitated in the author's own voice they are tells.

## No external biography this week

The thematic is scripture-driven end to end and names no external story. `story` and `insight` modules therefore draw on the biblical narrative the week is already in — Isaiah in the throne room, Moses in the cleft, Matthew's table — rather than on outside biography. This is a deliberate deviation from the usual one-verified-story-per-teaching-day, taken because inventing or thinly sourcing a biography would be worse than not having one.
