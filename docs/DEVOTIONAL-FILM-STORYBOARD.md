# REKINDLED — storyboard

**Status:** nothing rendered. This is the design document that should have existed
before a single frame was made.

**What went wrong the first time:** I built an asset schedule — one plate per
narration segment — and called it a timeline. That is not directing. A director
asks _what is the viewer seeing, shot by shot, and why does this shot cut to that
one._ I never asked it. The result was 27 wide shots held for ten minutes, which
is a slideshow with good taste.

---

## 1. The directorial idea

**The protagonist is the fire.** Not Abraham, not the narrator. Every chapter is
the same flame in a different condition, and the film is its biography.

| Ch  | Text           | State of the fire                   |
| --- | -------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| 1   | Genesis 1:1    | Burning before anyone arrives       |
| 2   | Genesis 15     | Walking the aisle alone             |
| 3   | 2 Timothy 1    | Hidden under ash                    |
| 4   | Exodus 29      | Steady on an altar, fed twice daily |
| 5   | 1 Samuel 3     | Nearly out                          |
| 6   | Matthew 5      | Raised up to be seen                |
| 7   | Leviticus 6:13 | Never going out                     |

Two things follow from that, and they are the whole design:

**Rule 1 — every chapter transition is a flame-to-flame match cut.** Same fire,
new circumstance. By chapter three the audience has learned the grammar, and from
then on the cuts carry the argument without narration having to state it.

**Rule 2 — the film bookends on a single frame.** Shot 1 is an extreme close-up
of a flame in blackness. The final image is the same flame at dawn. Everything in
between is what that fire did while nobody was awake.

---

## 2. The grammar

**Shot scale is the thing that was missing.** The first cut had exactly one
register — wide. A film breathes by changing distance.

`EWS` extreme wide · `WS` wide · `MS` medium · `CU` close · `ECU` extreme close · `INS` insert · `TYPE` typographic

Working ratio across the film: roughly **30% ECU/CU**, 30% MS, 25% WS/EWS, 15% type.
The closes are what the first cut had none of.

**Movement.** 1.05× across ninety seconds is invisible; that was the error. Moves
are now either _committed_ (a real push or lateral drift you can feel) or _locked_
(dead still, deliberately). Nothing in between — a half-hearted drift reads as
accident.

**Two registers, alternating:**

- **TEACH** (Wes Anderson) — locked, symmetrical, dead-centre, precise cuts on the
  sentence. Chapter cards. The diagram.
- **BREATHE** (Herzog) — committed slow moves, landscape, held past comfort.

**Average shot length target: ~8s.** Roughly 70 shots across 9:43. The first cut
had 27.

## 5. The audio — open, needs your call

You said it was terrible. I think I caused most of it.

**Likely cause, mine:** I time-stretched the narration to **0.85×** to slow the
read from 199 wpm to 169. A 15% stretch is well past where artifacts become
audible. That was a bad call — the professional fix for pace is the edit, not the
sample rate.

**Second cause, pre-existing:** `docs/decisions/VOICE-ROADMAP.md` already records
that this clone learned from an _interview_ — "responsive, reactive, rising
terminals" — and was then asked to read declarative prose.

**Options, cheapest first:**

1. **Drop the stretch entirely.** Re-render at natural pace and buy the slowness
   back with held frames and pauses between shots. Costs ~7k characters. Fixes the
   artifacting but not the underlying read.
2. **Re-render with different settings** — higher stability, and test `eleven_v3`
   if it is on the account. Still the same clone.
3. **You read it.** 1,266 words, one sitting. For a piece whose whole purpose is a
   quality bar, this is the honest answer.
4. **Fine-tune a local model** — the roadmap's own recommendation (F5-TTS /
   StyleTTS2 class). A real project, not a session.

My recommendation: **1 now so the storyboard can be cut against something, 3 for
the version that actually ships.**
