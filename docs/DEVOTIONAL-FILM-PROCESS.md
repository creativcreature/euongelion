# Devotional film — the process, and what it cost to learn it

**What this is:** the record of building _REKINDLED_, a long-form companion to the
seven-day series, from a devotional JSON to a rendered film. Written so the next
one takes a day instead of a night — including the parts that went wrong, because
those are the expensive knowledge.

---

## 1. The pipeline that works

```
devotional JSON  →  script  →  narration  →  timeline  →  metric gate  →  render
   (7 days)        (Claude)   (ElevenLabs)   (Claude)      (automatic)   (Remotion)
                                                 ↑
                              plates + cycles (Codex, free)
                                        ↑
                              STYLE REFERENCE PLATE attached
```

**Division of labour that held up:**

| Who            | Does                                            | Cost                                 |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| **Claude**     | Script, direction, timeline, spec, verification | Expensive — reserve for judgement    |
| **Codex**      | Images, components, asset generation            | **Free** on the ChatGPT subscription |
| **ElevenLabs** | Narration + original score                      | Existing Pro plan                    |
| **Remotion**   | Render                                          | Free, local                          |

The rule that emerged: **Claude writes the spec and judges the output; Codex
produces.** Production was most of the spend, and it is the part that delegates.

## 2. Assets — what to make, and how

### Plates (stills)

- Generated by **Codex** via `codex exec -s danger-full-access`, free, 1536×1024.
- **ALWAYS attach a style reference** with `-i <plate.png>`. This is the single
  biggest quality lever found. Cold text prompts drift off-brand immediately —
  the first flame sheets were asked for cold and came back as cartoon fire that
  had to be thrown away.
- Codex self-corrects: it caught its own ash reading "as rounded stones instead
  of fine ash" and redid it unprompted.

### Cycles (animated elements)

- One image = a **3×2 contact sheet**: the same object in six successive states.
  One generation guarantees identical style, scale and lighting across all six.
  Six separate generations drift and the flicker strobes.
- **Background must be pure black** for `mixBlendMode: screen` overlays. A cream
  background renders as a visible pale box over the plate. Learned the hard way.
- Slice in code via `background-size: 300% 200%` + stepped `background-position`.
- **Step on twos** — 12fps against a 30fps timeline, `Math.floor(frame/(30/12))`.
  Never interpolate. The friction between a stepped element and a smoothly
  drifting plate under it is where the handmade quality lives.

### Narration

- ElevenLabs, cloned voice, ~7.5k characters for a 7-minute film (~1.6% of a Pro
  month).
- **Never time-stretch.** Slowing 199→169 wpm with `atempo=0.85` introduced
  audible artifacting and was the single worst decision of the build. Pace is the
  edit's job, not the sample rate's.
- Voicebox is the free local alternative — profiles exist for both cloned and
  preset (Kokoro `am_michael`) voices at `127.0.0.1:17493`.

### Score

- **ElevenLabs Music** at `POST /v1/music` with `music_length_ms`. Works on the
  existing Pro plan.
- **Naming a composer trips a copyright filter** — "Bach", "Mozart", "Vivaldi",
  "Hans Zimmer" all return 400 _"prompt appears to have violated our Terms of
  Service"_. Describe the style instead: "sacred pipe organ and string orchestra,
  slow patient build, baroque cathedral, minor key, no percussion." Same result,
  no names.
- **Duck it.** A flat 0.24 bed buries the voice. Ride at 0.10 under narration and
  open to 0.20 in the gaps.

## 3. The film craft, learned by getting it wrong

Cut 1 was rejected in four words: _"literally nothing happens."_ The causes, all
real and all avoidable:

| Failure               | Cause                                                   | Fix                                                                               |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Nothing happens       | One shot per narration segment — 27 shots in 10 minutes | Punch-ins: `focus` + scale into a region of the SAME plate. 53 shots, no new art. |
| Flat and dead         | Every plate was a WIDE. No closes, no inserts.          | Target ≥30% close-ups. Cut 2 runs 50%.                                            |
| Invisible motion      | 1.00→1.05 across 90s cannot be perceived                | Moves are committed or locked. Nothing between.                                   |
| Blinking, not cutting | Every shot faded in from and out to black               | Hard cuts. Only explicit `black` shots touch black.                               |
| Slideshow feel        | Single flat image per shot                              | Layer classes: ground / subject / cycle / light / atmosphere / print              |

**The directorial idea that made it a film:** the protagonist is the fire. Every
chapter is the same flame in a different state — burning before anyone arrives,
walking the aisle alone, hidden under ash, steady on an altar, nearly out, raised
up, never going out. That makes match cuts _structural_ (every chapter hands to
the next flame-to-flame) rather than decorative, and it gives the film a bookend:
open on a flame in blackness, close on the same flame at dawn.

**Living misregistration.** Riso misregisters because paper shifts between passes.
Baked into a still, that is frozen. Drift the halftone screen 1–2px against the
ink on a slow sine and the film reads as a print being _pulled_ rather than a
photograph of a print. Two layers and a sine wave; no other channel can copy it
because it is a property of the medium.

**Storyboard first.** The first cut was an asset schedule — one plate per
narration row — not a shot list. A director asks what the viewer sees, shot by
shot, and why this shot cuts to that one. Skipping that is what produced a
slideshow with good taste.

## 4. The metric — success is measured, not asserted

`src/series/metrics.ts`, run with `npx tsx src/series/metrics.ts`. Scores the
timeline against published retention research. Every threshold cites its source,
and anything that is judgement rather than evidence is labelled as such.

|     | Check                              | Target   | Basis                                                          |
| --- | ---------------------------------- | -------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| R1  | Payoff promise lands               | ≤15s     | Steepest retention drop is 10–20s, inflection ~15s             |
| R2  | Pattern interrupt before first cut | ≤5s      | Three-phase hook: interrupt 0–5s, promise 5–15s, commit 15–30s |
| R3  | Opens on picture, not black        | —        | Long silence is a named cause of early drop-off                |
| R4  | Non-speech share                   | ≤20%     | High-retention long-form keeps density high                    |
| R5  | Dead air on a held image           | ≤8s      | Judgement — research names the failure, not a number           |
| R6  | Visual reset cadence               | 25–40s   | Resets every 10–20s in intro, widening once hooked             |
| R7  | Average shot length                | 8–12s    | Judgement inside the 5–10 cuts/min interview band              |
| R8  | Close-ups share                    | ≥30%     | Diagnosis of why cut 1 died                                    |
| R9  | Perceptible moves                  | ≥70%     | Diagnosis of why cut 1 died                                    |
| R10 | Shots with live animation          | ≥12      | Founder verdict on cut 1                                       |
| R11 | Runtime                            | 7–15 min | Suits comprehensive exploration if pacing holds                |

It caught two things a human review missed: the payoff promise landing at 0:17
against a 15s bar, and a bad proxy that counted deliberate chapter cards as "dead
air" when the same research _rewards_ them as visual resets.

## 5. Traps that cost real time

1. **Never render twice to the same filename.** A killed render plus a re-render
   plus a background process probing the file mid-mux produced a 1.3 GB file with
   157 decode errors that played blank. Render to a fresh name; verify before
   claiming anything.
2. **`nohup` inside a shell tool dies when the parent exits.** A render reported
   "complete" at 2,900 of 17,493 frames. Use tracked background execution.
3. **Verify by decoding, not by file size.** A stale file from another session sat
   at the expected path, right size, wrong content — 1080×1920 vertical, 38
   seconds, from the day before.
4. **Sampling a frame at a cut boundary looks like a bug.** A "black frame" at
   5:00 was local frame 0 of a shot with a fade-in. It did reveal a genuine flaw
   (every cut dipping through black), but the reported symptom was a measurement
   artefact.
5. **Mount narration outside the shot loop.** Inside a shot's `Sequence`, audio is
   clipped to that shot — a 40-second segment on a 12-second shot cuts off at 12.
6. **`partner_generate` has no image-to-video model.** Every registered video slug
   is text-to-video. Animating an existing plate needs `submit_workflow` with an
   OSS model, or After Effects.
7. **Determinism.** No `Math.random()`, no `Date.now()` in Remotion components, or
   frames differ between passes. Seed from index.

## 6. Costs

| Item                        | Cost                                                  |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Plates + components (Codex) | **£0** — ChatGPT subscription                         |
| Narration, ~7.5k chars      | ~1.6% of an ElevenLabs Pro month                      |
| Score, 3 original cues      | Same plan                                             |
| Render                      | Free, local, ~10 min for 7:39                         |
| Comfy                       | 1 job spent before realising Codex was the right door |

**A 7-minute film for effectively zero marginal cost.** The expensive input is
judgement, and that is the part worth spending on.
