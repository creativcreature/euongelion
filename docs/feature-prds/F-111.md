## Problem

> "the image crop/size needs to mirror the example i provided, sasme sixe and
> aspect ration (croping no skewing or stretching or nything). needs to overflow
> and repeat, and titles need to show. also needs to be contained within the
> newspaper boardeer."

F-110 had gone the other way: a varied mosaic, full-bleed, captions on hover.

## Four corrections

1. **Same size, same aspect.** Every plate is a fixed 4:3, cropped with
   `object-fit: cover` and never stretched or skewed. Measured: one unique tile
   size across the board, ratio 1.333.
2. **Overflow and repeat.** The catalog repeats four times so the board can be
   travelled rather than running out after two screens. Every tile is still a
   real series — it comes round again the way a pattern does. Nothing invented.
3. **Titles always show.** A hover-only name is invisible on a phone and to
   anyone scanning.
4. **Contained in the newspaper border** — 1308px inside a 1389px page on a
   1440px viewport. Full bleed was my invention; the border is the point of
   this site.

The packing algorithm is **deleted** along with the mosaic. A uniform grid
cannot have holes, so the three-stage hole-filling machinery and its test go
too, rather than sitting in the tree as dead weight.

## The homepage feature

> "homepage feature image should fill container. the text on the right should
> not have soo much - 10-14 words max. container height can be slightly
> shorter."

The art carried a **fixed height**, so whenever the copy column was taller the
plate stopped short of the row — that is the gap. It stretches to the row now
(with a floor so it cannot collapse), and the row's height is set by the copy.

The teaser went from **69 words to 12**. A featured slot is a headline and a
line, not a paragraph; the full introduction is one tap away on the series page.

Row height **530px → 291px**.

## Platform Contracts

### Desktop

- Feature: art stretches to the row; row driven by the copy column.

### Mobile

- Feature: stacked, so the art takes its own height again.
