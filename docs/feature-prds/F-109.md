## Problem

> "the Flow doesnt work atall"

And the outstanding mobile audit, which had been blocked three times by tooling.

## Flow scrolls natively

Measured, and it genuinely did not work:

- **one wheel notch threw the board 835px**, because the hand-written momentum
  loop stacked its own inertia on top of the operating system's
- **drag moved it zero pixels**

The transform-and-momentum machinery was the wrong foundation. The frame is now
an ordinary scroll container, and drag simply moves `scrollTop`. The platform
already has momentum, rubber-banding, scrollbars, keyboard paging and
accessibility — so there is far less here to break.

Verified: native scroll exact (300 → 300, no lurch), drag 1:1 (a 100px gesture
moves 100px), 37 tiles, 37/37 plates loaded.

The smear is now driven off **real scroll travel** rather than a velocity we
were guessing at, and settles 90ms after motion stops.

## Spines are responsive

The first genuine mobile measurement showed spine tap targets at **31px wide** —
under the 44px minimum, and far too narrow to read a title on. Seven to a shelf
is a *desktop* number: three on a phone, four on a small tablet, seven above
900px. Now 71px.

## Why the mobile audit took four attempts

Worth recording, because it cost hours:

- **The Chrome resize tool reports success and does not resize.** Every
  "mobile" check was actually running at desktop width.
- **The in-page JS tool can attach to a stale document** — it reported a 1313px
  viewport against a 414px window, and `clientWidth: 0` for an element that was
  visibly sized on screen.

**Playwright sets a real viewport and is the tool for this.**

## Platform Contracts

### Desktop

- Flow: `overflow-y: auto`, drag-to-pan at 1:1, blur ≤4px settling at rest.
- Spines: seven per shelf.

### Mobile

- Flow: pointer drag bails on `pointerType === 'touch'` — the platform owns touch.
- Spines: three per shelf under 560px, four under 900px.
