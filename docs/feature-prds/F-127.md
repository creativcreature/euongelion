## Shipped

### 36 · The public edition is in the tab bar

`/daily-bread` is the only fully public reading surface and had **no tab at
all**, while `TODAY` and `LIBRARY` are both auth-gated — a signed-out reader's
primary navigation was 40% sign-in wall.

It sits second, next to `TODAY`, because the two are the reading destinations
and belong together: `TODAY` is your plan, `DAILY BREAD` is the shared paper.
The icon is the paper itself — a sheet with its masthead rule and two columns.

The grid was `repeat(5, minmax(0, 1fr))`. It is now `grid-auto-flow: column`
with `grid-auto-columns: minmax(0, 1fr)`, so the bar adapts to whatever it
carries and the next change here does not need a magic number edited in two
places.

### 39 · The mobile menu is a dialog with real focus management

Three gaps, all closed:

- **It announced as an unnamed group.** Now `role="dialog"` with its label.
- **Focus never entered it.** A VoiceOver user tapped the hamburger and heard
  nothing — focus stayed on the toggle. Focus now moves to the first item on
  open.
- **Tab escaped into the page behind.** Tab and Shift-Tab now cycle within the
  panel.

Focus _return_ on close was already handled by the existing effect that focuses
`mobileToggleRef`.

**Deliberately not `aria-modal="true"`.** The page behind stays scrollable and
reachable — the scroll lock was reverted in F-118 because every variant broke
something worse, including `position: sticky` on the topbar. Claiming inertness
that does not exist would be a lie to assistive technology.

### 51 · Tabular figures where digits stack

`.rr-count`, `.cover-meta`, `.issue-meta` and `.preview-kicker` — the series
counter and the per-card day counts, the places a number repeats down a column
and shimmers as it changes. `.fp-desk-count` and `.stock-meta` already had it.

Deliberately **not** global: `.oldstyle-nums` is a real editorial choice for
numerals inside prose and must not be overridden.

### 52 · Balanced headlines

`text-wrap: balance` on the ten title and standfirst classes, so a short heading
stops orphaning its last word. Scoped to headings and deks — on running body
copy it is both wrong and expensive.
