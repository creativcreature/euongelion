## User Expectation

The founder promised a colleague a reading on a named theme by a named date. She
asked for **healing** and **being patient**; he added **rest**. He then spoke a
five-minute reading on the spot. The expectation is not "a devotional about those
three words." It is that the thing he said out loud arrives written down, at full
production quality, with a link he can send.

Two audiences have to be served by one artifact:

1. **The person it was written for.** Worn down, in a season that has taken
   capacity, and — on the evidence of the same conversation — inclined to read a
   hard season as something being done _to_ her.
2. **Every other reader.** The reading is in the public catalog. It cannot depend
   on knowing who it was for, and it must not name or describe her.

## Content design

**Anchors.** The two-minute open anchors in Isaiah 40:28-31 (KJV); the deep dive
anchors in Hebrews 12:1-2 (KJV). KJV is chosen deliberately in both places: it
renders ὑπομονή (_hypomonē_) as **patience** — the exact word asked for — where
modern versions render it _endurance_, and "Looking unto Jesus" carries the
directional force of ἀφορῶντες (_aphorōntes_) better than "fix our eyes on."

**The lexical architecture** is the teaching payload, and all three words are
verified:

- ὄγκος (_ogkos_, G3591) — bulk, mass. Weight, named separately from sin.
- εὐπερίστατος (_euperistatos_, G2139) — hapax legomenon; Thayer's reconstructs
  "skilfully surrounding… to prevent or retard running" and notes it is not found
  elsewhere. Presented as the reconstruction it is.
- ἀφοράω (_aphoraō_, G872) — "to look away from all else at one object." Two NT
  occurrences; present participle in Hebrews 12:2, so continuing action.
- περισπάω (_perispaō_, G4049) — "to drag all around," one NT occurrence, Luke
  10:40, **imperfect passive** — done to Martha, not chosen by her.

Three of these describe the same geometry: surrounded, encircled, pulled from
every direction — and the answer given is not to fight on every side but to look
at one thing. That is the founder's sun image, arrived at lexically.

**Where it resolves.** The risk in this brief is writing a "try harder" reading
to someone who asked for rest. Isaiah 40:31 puts the running _after_ the waiting;
1 Kings 19 gives Elijah two meals and two sleeps before a single word; Milton's
sonnet answers the day-labour complaint with "God doth not need either man's work
or his own gifts." Rest is the thing the race is run on, not the prize for
finishing it.
