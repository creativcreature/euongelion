# F-128: The intro stops flashing, and the parallax gains weight

## Metadata

- Feature ID: F-128
- Category: Motion
- Owner: founder
- Status: shipped (2026-08-18)
- Decision: SA-082
- Surfaces: `/` (masthead intro), site-wide scroll motion

## Problem

> "The intro animation is a bit misstimed and the color transition looks like a
> glitch on the words. It is white overlayed over the blue masthead breifly for
> a frame or so and it looks like a mistake. Site wide animation paralaxing and
> such needs more finesse and innovation."

## 1. The white-over-blue flash was a timing overlap, not a colour bug

Both timelines start together the moment `.is-lifting` lands:

| Layer                   | Timing                     | Ends at    |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------- | ---------- |
| `.press-ink` retract    | `transform 900ms`          | **900ms**  |
| `.press-word--key` fade | `opacity 260ms ease 820ms` | **1080ms** |

So for **180ms** the cream wordmark was still painted over a page whose real
cobalt masthead had already been uncovered. Cream letters on top of cobalt
letters — exactly the flash the founder describes.

It landed on the wordmark specifically because `.press-ink` collapses toward
`top center`, so the masthead band is the **last** thing it uncovers. The
overlap could not have happened anywhere harmless.

**Fix:** the fade now completes at **840ms**, 60ms before the ink finishes
(`opacity 240ms ease 600ms`). The cream word is gone before the real one is
revealed, and the two never share a frame.

## 2. The intro lingered after it was over

The sequence finishes at ~2050ms — hand-off at 1050ms, flight 1000ms — but the
hard stop sat at 3200ms, leaving ~1.15s of a fully transparent, inert overlay
over a finished page. Invisible, and still the difference between an intro that
ends and one that lingers. Now 2250ms.

## 3. Parallax has inertia instead of tracking the scrollbar

The old loop mapped scroll position straight to a transform, once per scroll
event. That is why it read mechanical: the layer was rigidly pinned to the
scrollbar and started and stopped in the same frame the finger did, with no
weight to it.

Each layer now **eases toward** its target at a fixed per-frame rate, so it
leads slightly into a scroll and settles after one — the difference between a
thing being dragged and a thing having mass.

Three properties preserved deliberately:

- **The ±28px cap is unchanged.** Depth, not drift; nothing detaches from its
  caption.
- **The first frame snaps to target.** An element seen for the first time is
  already where it belongs — no sliding in from zero on load.
- **The loop parks itself.** It runs while anything is still moving and shuts
  down after 24 still frames, so a stationary page costs nothing. The old
  version did no work at rest either; this keeps that property despite now
  being a continuous animation rather than an event handler.

`prefers-reduced-motion` still disables the whole system before the loop starts.

## What was considered and rejected

A velocity-driven **registration slip** — a crimson ghost offsetting a pixel or
two on fast scroll and settling, echoing the misregistration the intro is built
from. It is the most on-brand motion idea available and it was not shipped:
it needs a pseudo-element on each plate, and `::after` collisions have already
cost this codebase two bugs in two days (F-120's hit pads over the switcher,
fixed in F-121). Worth doing deliberately, with its own decision, not as a
side effect of a timing fix.

## Contracts

### Desktop

- No frame in which the intro wordmark and the real masthead are both painted.
- Parallax travel stays within ±28px and settles when scrolling stops.

### Mobile

- Same; the intro is skipped entirely under reduced motion.
