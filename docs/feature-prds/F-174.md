## Problem

Founder, 2026-08-28, listing nine constructions that read as machine-written:
the seesaw ("That's not X. That's Y."), fragment pairs, two-picture metaphors
with no advice, self-applause, the X-of-Y analogy, throat-clearing, forced
triads, hedged ranges, and the recap ending.

The existing forbidden-phrase list caught only `it's not X, it's Y` in its
comma-joined single-sentence form. The period-split variant — the one that
actually appears in prose — passed straight through.

## Solution

The nine tells documented in `AUTHORING-SPEC.md` §2 as pattern → why → fix,
mirrored into `AI-CONTENT-CONSTRAINTS.md` so the two docs cannot drift, and
enforced in `validate-devotional.mjs` by a new `AI_TELLS` tier.

**Founder exemption:** "Unless you are directly quoting scripture or an actual
human written piece, then it's avoidable." Antithetical parallelism and triads
are everywhere in Scripture ("I am not come to destroy, but to fulfil"; "Holy,
holy, holy") — quoted they are content, in the author's own voice they are
tells. The scan therefore runs against QUOTE-STRIPPED prose.

**Severity is NEEDS-FIX, not BLOCKING** — these are rewrites, not build breaks.
devo-go's loop already runs to 0 BLOCKING / 0 NEEDS-FIX, so they still gate a
new series without failing anything else.

**Forward-only in practice**: the validator is invoked per-slug on the series
just written, so the back catalogue is never in scope.

Four tells stay editorial rather than mechanical — fragment pairs, the
two-picture metaphor, the X-of-Y analogy and manufactured triads all fire on
legitimate devotional prose.
