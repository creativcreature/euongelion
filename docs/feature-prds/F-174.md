## Problem

The weekly series machine's discovery pass surfaced a recurring human
condition across the last month of faith/culture conversation: bone-deep,
isolated exhaustion — the conviction that a person is carrying everything
alone, and that quitting quietly would be reasonable rather than dramatic.
1 Kings 19 answers it point for point, in the order the text actually gives
it: collapse right after triumph, a meal and sleep before a single word of
counsel, a mountain where the drama gets dismissed and the near-silence gets
the visit, a repeated question answered with a task rather than an argument,
and a number — seven thousand — as the direct reply to "I am the only one
left."

## What the text does

Sunday-start week shape (SA-029 sabbath-first): Day 1 sabbath, Days 2-6 the
deep-dive chiasm A-B-C-B′-A′, Day 7 recap.

- **Day 1 (sabbath)** — Psalm 23:1-3 (KJV). No teaching; rest stated before
  the week's difficulty begins.
- **Day 2 (A)** — 1 Kings 19:1-5a. Collapse the day after Elijah's greatest
  public victory. The double bind (admitting exhaustion feels like either
  failure or overreaction) is named once, plainly, then the text is taught.
  Meta-story: KINGDOM (Ahab/Jezebel, mid-9th century BC). Cross-testament:
  James 5:17.
- **Day 3 (B)** — 1 Kings 19:5b-8. God's first response is a meal and sleep,
  twice, before any word. Cross-testament: Mark 6:31.
- **Day 4 (C, pivot)** — 1 Kings 19:9-13. Wind, earthquake, and fire named
  and dismissed; the phrase behind "a still, small voice" verified as more
  literally "a voice, a thin silence" — a paradox, not simply gentle.
  Cross-testament: Hebrews 1:1-3.
- **Day 5 (B′)** — 1 Kings 19:13-17. The identical question and complaint,
  twice; God answers with a road, three names, and a successor rather than a
  rebuke. Cross-testament: John 21:15-17.
- **Day 6 (A′)** — 1 Kings 19:18, with Romans 11:1-5 as the anchor's own
  cross-testament reading (Paul quotes this exact exchange directly).
- **Day 7 (recap)** — the week walked back in order, plus Further-Your-
  Learning: verified videos, primary texts, a passage trail, a guided read
  of 1 Kings 17-19.

## Verification

- All scripture pulled verbatim from the `public/bibles/` corpus.
- Four parallel research agents (stories / quotes / Hebrew+context / videos)
  fetched primary or scholarly-triangulated sources directly. Key finds: the
  rothem broom tree is _Retama raetam_, a largely leafless desert shrub —
  never a canopy shade tree (KJV's "juniper" is a mistranslation); _demamah_
  (H1827) is one of the rarest nouns in the Hebrew Bible, occurring only in
  1 Kings 19:12 and Job 4:16; the "fine as dust" golden-calf parallel is
  Deuteronomy 9:21, not Exodus 32:20.
- Five stories verified: Spurgeon's own depression (primary lecture text),
  Newton's care for Cowper (secondary scholarly citation of his diary, held
  with that caveat), Pascal's Memorial (primary Gutenberg translation),
  Colson/Prison Fellowship (memoir + interview), Brother Andrew (used to show
  the frequency of Elijah's exact false belief, not as a tight personal
  parallel).
- Quotes verified verbatim against fetched primary sources: Spurgeon
  ("Elijah Fainting," "The Still Small Voice"), Alexander Maclaren
  ("Elijah's Weakness, and Its Cure," cross-checked across three independent
  digitizations), Dietrich Bonhoeffer (_Life Together_).
- Videos oEmbed- and embed-verified. Three slots (burnout, recommissioning,
  remnant) have no verified official video in BibleProject's catalog —
  shipped without an inline video rather than a mismatched substitute.
