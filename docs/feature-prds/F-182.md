## Problem

Founder, 2026-09-10:

> "I want the audio player more like audible. Right now its hard to navigate on
> mobile — especially when I am driving. Audio presentation and feature needs
> help."

Then, on the first pass at the design:

> "These desig. Currentky doenst looke like rhe website."

And two corrections that each changed the build:

> "I need to be beable to switch chapters etc in drive mode."
> "Scrubber needs an better touchpoint so I can easily slide to specific section"
> "The line is too thick- needs a thinner rule."

Measured in the shipped build, the report was literal:

| | Before |
| --- | --- |
| Transport order | back · play · forward · **next · previous** |
| Target sizes | all five at 44px, only the play GLYPH larger |
| Smallest labels | `.lsn-context` **8px**, `.lsn-eyebrow` 8.5px, `.lsn-dur` 9.3px, `.lsn-times` 9.9px |
| Seeking | one `<input type="range">` across a whole reading |
| Media Session | 4 handlers — no seek, no `setPositionState`, no artwork |
| Docked bar | no dismiss, and hidden on the reading it was playing |

At 350px wide a 21-minute reading is **3.6 seconds per pixel**, with chapter
marks about 15px apart.

## Solution

### The section rule

A 2px ruled track with a 26px thumb, a 56px touch strip, snap-to-section and
full keyboard operation. Two earlier shapes were built and rejected, and both
look right on paper:

- a prev/now/next **rail** with no scrubber — removed the ability to reach an
  arbitrary section at all;
- a **segmented** bar — segments need ~9px to stay hittable, real sections run
  from 6s to 150s, so proportional segments made short ones under 2px and a
  floor made the bar stop showing real time.

The founder's "too thick" note is what resolved it: ticks need no width, so the
rule went to 2px and the track became **true linear time**. Thinness bought
accuracy, not just restraint.

### Tiered ticks — the premise, and the thing the first plan got wrong

The design asks a listener to navigate by section NAME. The original claim was
that Euangelion could beat a podcast app because its sections carry editorial
names rather than "Chapter 4". Measured over all **6,132 chapter marks**, that
is true of 44% of them.

**3,451 marks — 56.3% — are module labels**: Scripture 687, Word study 590,
Reflect 592, Opening 568, Prayer 520, Takeaway 487, plus 4 "Reflection" and 3
"Title". And **13% of readings repeat a label**; `bible-365-day-1` says
"Scripture" seven times.

So `isStructuralChapter()` splits them. An editorial heading takes a tall tick
and owns the headline; a module label takes a short tick and carries a timecode,
which is what tells three Scriptures apart.

**Two readings have only one editorial chapter** — `all-these-things-day-7` and
`drawing-near-day-7`, both day-7 sabbath readings whose single non-module
heading is "Sabbath". That is a true property of a short liturgical reading, not
a gap in the tier list; narrowing the list further would only un-tier Prayer or
Scripture. `__tests__/audio-chapter-tiers.test.ts` pins both slugs so a third
going thin fails the build.

### The car

`GlobalAudioHost` registered four Media Session handlers. It now also registers
`seekbackward`, `seekforward` and `seekto`, reports position through
`setPositionState`, and sets artwork from `coverForReading`. Until this there
was **no skip-back anywhere on a lock screen, a steering-wheel control or a head
unit**, the OS progress bar was empty, and the artwork square was blank.

`seekto` is deliberately NOT snapped to a section: the in-app rule snaps, the OS
bar is continuous, and rounding a driver's own nudge would give them a jump they
did not ask for.

### Drive mode

Media Session cannot give a web app a browsable list in the car — CarPlay and
Android Auto show artwork, metadata, a seek bar and five buttons, and nothing
more, without a native shell. So the section list lives on the phone: the rule
at the top (sliding beats scrolling while moving), every section as a 68px row
beneath it, ±15 at 96px, play at 132px, Escape leaves, and a wake lock so the
screen does not sleep mid-drive.

### Type from the ladder

The first plan proposed an 11px floor. That was itself a defect: SA-092 says
"any font-size that is not one of these tokens is a defect", and
`typography-craft.css` already ships a 17px reading floor. Every size now comes
off `--ts-*`, and a test fails the build if a literal rem or a literal hex
appears in any listening surface.

## Contract

### Desktop

The drawer remains a right sidebar at `min(26rem, 92vw)` with a 3px gold left
edge. The transport runs previous-section · back-15 · play · forward-15 ·
next-section at 54 / 60 / 78px. The section rule replaces the whole-track range
input; volume, share, the queue with reorder/remove, save-as-playlist and the
OccasionPicker are unchanged. Drive mode is reachable but aimed at a phone.

### Mobile

The drawer stays a bottom sheet under 768px. Every target is at least 54px and
nothing sets type below `--ts-xs`. The docked bar names the SECTION on its first
line with the reading beneath it, carries a 2px progress rule, stays visible on
the reading it is playing, and can be dismissed without clearing the queue —
dismissal is session-only and resets whenever something new is played. Volume
stays hidden under `@media (pointer: coarse)` because iOS Safari ignores
`audio.volume`.

## Deliberately not done

- **A "no chapters" empty state.** All 571 rendered tracks carry chapters
  (median 10; 442 have exactly ten). `SectionRule` returns null below two
  because the prop is public, and the drawer falls back to the plain range input
  for that case — losing the ability to seek entirely would be a far worse
  failure than the plainer control.
- **Word-level read-along.** Needs per-word timings the render pipeline does not
  emit.
- **Trim silence.** A permanent no: the renderer's `PAUSE_AFTER` grammar is
  content.

## Open, and the founder's to settle

- **Lock-screen mapping.** `title` currently carries the SECTION and `artist`
  the reading, so the biggest text in a car names where you are. Swapping the
  two lines in `GlobalAudioHost` reverses it; nothing else depends on the choice.
- **Whether narration is disclosed as synthesised.** Open since the August
  research and untouched here.

## Follow-up: the audio stopped on a tab switch (2026-09-12)

Founder, from the installed PWA: "when in web app (save to ios) and I switch
tabs- the audio stops."

Not a tab-switch bug. `MobileTabBar` uses `next/link`, and the one `<audio>`
element lives in the root layout (SA-115), so a route change cannot reach it.
`ServiceWorkerRegistration` reloaded the page unconditionally on
`controllerchange`, and a reload destroys the document and the element with it.
`registration.update()` runs on mount and a waiting worker is promoted at once,
so any update landing mid-reading killed playback. A standalone PWA has no
browser chrome to make a reload legible, so it reads as the audio stopping.

The reload now defers while the element is playing and re-arms on `pause` and
`ended`. A reader who is not listening sees exactly the previous behaviour —
pinned by two of the six tests in `__tests__/sw-reload-audio-guard.test.tsx`, so
the deferral cannot become a way to get stuck on a stale build.

- [x] A controllerchange mid-playback does not reload
- [x] The reload lands on pause and on ended, once only
- [x] Nothing playing, or no element at all, still reloads immediately
- [ ] **UNVERIFIED — the iOS platform half.** Backgrounding a standalone PWA can
      suspend audio regardless of application code; the mitigation is the fully
      populated Media Session this release also ships. Which of the two the
      founder hit needs the phone to settle.
