## No source exists for our editions — verified, not assumed

| Source | Attribution? | Covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `eng-kjv.osis.xml` (on disk) | **Yes** — 2,081 `<q who="Jesus">` milestone pairs, verse-addressed | 1 of 125 Gospel passages directly |
| BSB official USFM (public domain) | **No** — `43LUKBSB.usfm`, 156 KB, zero `\wj` | 70 of 125 |
| NIV | Copyrighted, no open dataset | 50 of 125 |
| STEPBible-Data (on disk) | No speaker attribution | — |

So the attribution was **built**, by cross-referencing the KJV OSIS onto BSB
wording verse by verse — KJV and BSB share verse boundaries, so the verse is the
join key and no similarity matching is needed.

## Why inference is not acceptable

Luke 10:33-37 (BSB), a live passage in `looking-at-the-sun-day-4`, has three
quoted spans. The middle — "The one who showed him mercy" — is the expert in the
law. A quotation-mark pass puts Christ's colour on another man's words. KJV
compounds it by not punctuating speech at all.

**Standing principle:** a missing red word is a typographic omission; a wrongly
red word is a false attribution. Under-mark, never over-mark.

## The three resolution paths

1. **Whole-verse (translation-independent).** The KJV marks the entire verse as
   His, so the whole passage is His in *any* translation. This is what brings
   NIV/ESV/NKJV into red letter at all — Matthew 16:24 is "Whoever wants to be
   my disciple…" in NIV and "If anyone wants to come after Me…" in BSB, and
   verbatim matching cannot bridge that. 1,427 verses.
2. **Bare saying.** A single verse whose saying closes it, quoted without its
   reporting clause or quote marks — the excerpt *is* the saying. Guarded: a
   passage still carrying quote marks or a reporting verb is excluded. 522
   verses.
3. **Verbatim span.** BSB-worded spans matched literally in the passage. Only
   fires where the wording matches, so an unmappable translation stays black.

Ambiguous verses (27) are refused outright.

## Colour is measured, not chosen

`#7a1c12` is the deepest red clearing WCAG AA (≥4.5:1) against **every**
highlight ground:

```
yellow 7.8 · blue 5.4 · green 6.4 · pink 5.6 · purple 5.0 · cream 9.1
```

Dark mode unhighlighted lifts to `#f5988a` (7.0:1). **Inside a highlight it
returns to `#7a1c12` in both themes**, because highlight grounds are light
pastels in both — the founder's "ensure the highlight color is correct for such
text", and the reason the light value was measured against the swatches rather
than the page.

Editorial emphasis renders **inside** the red rather than being replaced by it —
attribution is who is speaking, emphasis is what the devotional points at.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] Attribution built from a real source, never inferred

- [x] Whole-verse sayings redden in any translation

- [ ] Deployed and live-verified

## Platform Contracts

### Desktop

- `.wj` renders inline within the passage at the same size and family as the
  surrounding scripture — colour is the only differentiator, as in a print
  red-letter edition. `.wj-emphasis` nests inside it for editorial emphasis.
- Selection over red letter keeps the red rather than inheriting the site's
  selection ink.

### Mobile

- No layout change: red letter is a colour on existing inline text, so it adds
  no width, no wrapping change and no touch target at any breakpoint.
- Print stylesheet keeps `#7a1c12`, matching a physical red-letter edition.

## Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| A wrongly-red word is a false attribution | Three guarded paths; 27 ambiguous verses refused; negative control in test. |
| A future translation swap silently drops red | Verbatim path fails closed (black), and the apply test asserts catalog coverage stays >50%. |
| The 239 KB dataset reaching the client | `red-letter-resolve` is server/build only; only resolved spans travel. |
