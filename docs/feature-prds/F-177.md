## Problem

Founder, referencing portal.nousresearch.com: a dithered background image behind
the reading that changes as you scroll, using the same plates that appear in the
page, parallaxing behind a long section and then leaving breathing room before
the next one. Typography must stay contrasted against it.

Scope was explicit and narrow: **this series only.** "Dont touch any other
devotional layouts or look and feel or anything."

## Solution

A fixed canvas behind the article, mounted only when the slug begins
`drawing-near-`. Bands are anchored on the plates that actually appear in the
page, so the texture behind you is always the image you are reading toward.

Rendering reuses the SA-128 halftone engine unchanged — it RESAMPLES (averages
into coarse cells, one dot per cell) rather than re-quantising per pixel, which
is what keeps it free of moiré on plates that are already riso halftone.

Each plate is rasterised **once** into an offscreen canvas; the scroll handler
only translates it. Per-frame cost is one drawImage regardless of dot count, and
the handler is coalesced to a single rAF (SA-129).

## Two corrections that shaped it

**It flooded the page grey.** These are intensity-5 plates — about 79% deep ink
— so "dark source = big dot" closed nearly every cell. Measured on the first
pass: 12,914 of 13,360 sampled pixels painted, with type on a grey wash.

The first fix was a hard shadow gate: print only the deepest shadows. It stopped
the flood and lost the picture. Founder: _"I want to see the actual image not
just the halftone texture... way too large and subtle."_

So the range is **compressed rather than clipped** — a gamma above 1 pushes
midtones down, a ceiling holds the darkest cells below solid. Every tone still
varies, so the subject reads, and no cell is ever full. The screen went from
cell 18 to cell 7 and the crop from 2.3x to 1.25x past cover for the same
reason: at a coarse screen and a hard crop you see a texture, not the image.

**The plate was being stretched, not cropped.** `sampleGrid` fits its source to
the whole output, so handing it the plate directly squashed it and showed the
entire composition at once. It is now drawn into a correctly-shaped staging
canvas first.

## Contrast

The texture is **erased from behind the reading measure** and kept at strength in
the margins, rather than the whole layer being faded until it disappears. That is
what the reference does — texture at the edges, clean ground where the words are
— and it buys contrast without losing the effect.

Blend mode is chosen from the measured luminance of the live reader theme
(multiply on cream, screen on navy and black), because the reader ships themes at
both ends.

## Contract

### Desktop

`/devotional/drawing-near-day-{1..7}` only. Four swells with three gaps of clean
paper down day 1, measured every 900px: header plate fades by 1800; nothing
until 4500; plate 1 through 8100; nothing until 10800; plate 2; nothing; plate 3.

### Mobile

Same component; the carve-out is a proportion of viewport width, so the reading
measure stays clean at narrow widths. Parallax is disabled entirely under
`prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Scope proof

Verified rather than asserted: in the same build, `all-these-things-day-1` has
zero `.drawing-near-atmosphere` nodes, `.devotional-shell-main` keeps
`z-index: auto`, and the page renders identically to before.

      reader theme (screen blend) as well as cream (multiply).
- [x] Intro plate anchored to the reading itself — starts behind the first
      scripture, gone by the reflection. Verified: 0 ink above it, 16.5 at the
      start, 30.1 at the midpoint, 0 by the reflection.

      offsets at mount, which went stale once plates loaded and the article grew.
