# Version 6 — Paper Theatre

**Concept.** The parable of the sower staged as cut-paper stop motion: every element is a
torn paper shape with a fibrous edge and a soft cast shadow, hinged at paper joints and
jittering on twos like a real rig, printed in the house inks on warm paper.

**References.** Lotte Reiniger's silhouette theatre and paper-collage stop motion, with the
riso print texture of the Euangelion plates. The pace is pushed toward the founder's
reference films.

**Loop.** 22.0 s, seamless (the curtain closes on one seed and opens on the same field).
19 shots, so a cut every 1.16 s on average.

## Measured pace

- **19 cuts** in 22.0 s → **0.86 cuts per second**, average shot 1.16 s.
- **~53 discrete visual events** (cuts plus entries, transformations, punches, flashes)
  → **about 2.4 events per second**.
- Longest interval with no discrete change: **~1.3 s**, inside the two breaths — and even
  there the frame is never still (feather turning, wheat swaying, paper jitter on twos).
- Bursts of 3–4 events per second at the throw (2.8–3.3 s), the snatch (5.9–6.3 s) and the
  hundredfold (17.9–18.8 s).

This is faster than the brief's 1–1.5 per second average, but the cut rate itself is under
one per second, so it reads as energetic rather than strobing.

## Site fit

- 3:1 front band and 2:1 phone band; horizontal framing is held equal on the narrower band.
- No words, letters or numerals. Counts are shown as clusters of ears, never digits.
- No images, video or data URIs: everything is drawn by code each frame.
- Cobalt, gold and crimson on warm paper; overprints give the green sprout and the orange sun.
- Standalone HTML with the capture contract; 30 fps cap, DPR cap 1.5, reduced motion shows a still.

## Honest weaknesses

1. **The shapes are simple.** Compared with the fruit fly film there is far less drawing per
   shot: paper cut-outs carry silhouette and motion, not fine detail.
2. **The seed's flight (4.5–5.4 s) is still the thinnest stretch** — a few seeds settling and
   a shadow crossing. It reads, but it is the least interesting second and a half.
3. **The sower appears in only three shots.** The second half has no character in it, so the
   energy comes from growth and light rather than performance.
4. **The wheat ears are crude** — stacked ovals on a stalk. They read as wheat in the band,
   but not close up.
5. **Phone performance is unmeasured.** Only checked on an M4 Pro; the shadow blur on every
   paper piece is the likely cost. UNVERIFIED on older phones.
