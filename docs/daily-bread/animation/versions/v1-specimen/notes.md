# Version 1: Specimen Plate

**Concept.** The parable of the sower told as a natural-history plate: the life of one wheat grain, drawn and examined like a botanist's specimen, but with no words and no people.

**References.**
- The Opus 5 fruit-fly film: specimen close-ups, construction lines that draw themselves on, and the cut from macro detail to diagram.
- 19th-century botanical and entomological engravings.
- Euangelion's cobalt, gold and crimson riso inks.

**Loop.** 24 s, seamless: the last frame is the first frame, the same grain, upright.

Bursts of about 3 per second land on the bird's strike, the scorch, the thorns closing and the hundredfold. Two breaths carry the emotional weight: the good soil at 13.95–15.15 s and the ripe ear at 18.9–19.3 s. Nothing is ever fully still: the paper grain boils, gold motes drift, draft circles turn, ruled skies shift, rain falls and the worm moves.

## Shot list

1. **0–4.7 s: The specimen.** The grain punches in, a draft circle whips around it, the section cuts open, five callout rules fire out, two magnifier lenses pop in (the seed coat's layers; the starch cells), the grain flips, the camera punches to the embryo with a crimson flash, pulls back, everything is struck off, and the grain divides into four.
2. **4.7–15.7 s: The four soils.** Four grains arc out and four lenses snap open. The camera punches into each lens in turn, tells it, and cuts back out.
   - **Path (5.3–7.3):** a sparrow hops in, pecks, snatches the grain and flies off; a feather drifts down.
   - **Rocky (7.5–9.5):** a sprout springs up between stones, the hatched sun swells with crimson heat, and the sprout scorches and wilts.
   - **Thorns (9.7–11.7):** three thorn stems whip in and close on the seedling.
   - **Good soil (11.9–15.15):** rain, the grain splits, roots branch, two shoots rise, then a breath.
   - The three failures are struck through, and the fourth lens opens out.
3. **15.9–19.6 s: The ear.** The stalk whips up, leaves snap out, the ear builds spikelet by spikelet, awns fire out, a ripening sweep runs bottom to top, the halo pulses, the camera punches in, a gust passes, and it cuts out.
4. **19.6–24 s: A hundredfold.** The grains burst off the ear and settle into rosettes of 30, 60 and 100, each with a camera punch and a pulse, then spin, gather, and become the one grain from the first frame.

## Site fit

- **Front band.** Composed for 3:1, with all four lenses side by side.
- **Phone.** At 2:1 the lenses form a 2×2 grid; the camera punches make each beat read large on a small screen.
- **Rules met.** No text or numerals; nothing loaded (every frame drawn by code); the test-04 print engine (halftone tone plate, solid line plate, misregistration, grain); 30 fps cap; DPR cap 1.5; pauses off screen; reduced motion shows a still.
- **Cost.** A frame takes about 2–12 ms to draw on an M4 Pro. Phone cost is UNVERIFIED.

## What changed in the re-cut

- The loop dropped from 26 s to 24 s while gaining about 30 more events.
- Every soil is now a camera punch-in rather than a wide view of four small circles, so each beat reads large.
- Story beats were compressed: the sparrow's whole sequence now runs 2 s instead of 5; the scorch 2 s instead of 4.5.
- Added: the flip and embryo punch, the crimson flash, the strike-outs on the three failures, ring pulses on the fold diagrams, a spin-and-gather finish.
- Added constant micro-motion so nothing is dead: drifting motes, boiling rules, turning draft circles, a shifting ruled sky, rain and the worm.

## Honest weaknesses

- **Thorns (weakest).** Still smooth geometric spirals with triangle barbs. At the new speed they read better as an action, but they are not a believable briar.
- **The 1.2 s good-soil breath** is the one moment that could feel slow next to the fly film; it is deliberate, but it is the first thing I would cut further.
- **The fold rosettes** are clear as a diagram but emotionally flat; the pulses help, but the grains are small.
- **Craft gap.** Line-work density per shot is still well below the fly film's.
