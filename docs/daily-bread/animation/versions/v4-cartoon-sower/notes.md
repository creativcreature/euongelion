# Version 4: The Cartoon Sower (re-cut for pace)

**Concept.** The parable told as a mid-century studio cartoon, printed as a three-colour screenprint: a geometric, big-nosed sower flings seed across four soils, and each soil answers with its own gag. Re-cut from one slow tracking shot into 28 cuts of close-ups, mediums and wides.

## References
- **"Get Up" salon cards** (the founder's book photo): flat ink blocks on cream, rough edges, dry-brush voids, overprints, faces made of a few paper-coloured knockout lines.
- **Mid-century UPA and studio cartoons:** geometric design, rubber-hose limbs, anticipation, snap, squash and stretch, held reaction, cut.
- **The Opus 5 fruit-fly film:** the cutting rate and the habit of never holding a static frame.

## Measured pace
- **Cuts:** 28 in 22 s, **1.27 per second**. Median gap between cuts **0.65 s**; the longest cut-to-cut gap inside the action is 1.9 s (the wide at 16.3, which pans while the field sways, birds hop and he strolls off).
- **All visual events** (cuts, snaps, entrances, transformations, gag beats, takes): 59 in 22 s, **2.7 per second**; median gap **0.32 s**, longest **1.1 s**.
- **Bursts** run 2–3 events per second on the gags (the fling, the gulp, the sun's grin, the squeeze, each boing). Four deliberate holds sit on reaction faces (0.6–0.7 s each), where the acting is the event: the double take, the wince, the shock with the hat pop, the delight.
- **Nothing is static:** ink edges boil, clouds bob, cloth and sack swing, he breathes and blinks every ~2.6 s, wheat sways, perched birds hop and blink.

## What changed in the re-cut
1. **Cut list replaces the tracking shot.** He no longer walks the band in real time; the camera cuts between close-ups and wides and jumps him to each soil.
2. **Close-ups carry the story:** his face (four reaction shots), the bird's smug gulp and wink, the sun's grin, the sprout boings, and the thorn squeeze at 6× zoom.
3. **The thorn gag is fixed.** A push-in fills the frame: vines whip in, coil, and squeeze in 0.3 s with impact strokes, then the plant slumps burnt.
4. **More face.** A bigger eye, blinks, brows, and four mouths (smile, grin, flat, O); the hat pops on the shock take; a crimson smear trails the fling.
5. **Faster physics.** Seeds fly in ~0.3 s and bounce on the path; sprouts pop with overshoot; wheat rises in 0.55 s per stalk.
6. **Legibility:** rocks are knocked out of the soil with a weighted outline; medium shots frame him head to foot; hills are dropped from close-ups.

## How it fits the site
- **Bands.** Built for the 3:1 front band; composes at 2:1 by widening every shot (checked at 780×390).
- **No imagery.** Nothing is loaded; every frame is drawn by code. No words, letters or numerals.
- **Inks.** Cobalt #1a4d98, gold #efbc3f, crimson #ce3442 on paper #ebdcb2, each on its own slightly misregistered screen.
- **Page contract.** `?capture=1&w=&aspect=` exposes `__render`, `__loop` (22) and `__ready`. Normal mode caps 30 fps and DPR 1.5, pauses off-screen bands, and shows one still under reduced motion.

## Honest weaknesses
- **The thorn squeeze still reads as shapes, not as pain.** The coil is clear now, but the plant has no face, so the beat lands on impact strokes rather than performance.
- **The walk-off at 16.3–18.2** is the quietest stretch. It has sway, hops and a pan, but it is the weakest 2 seconds.
- **The face is still simple**: one eye, a brow, a mouth. It carries close-ups, but the drawing is plain beside the reference films.
- **Jump cuts** move him between soils with no travel. Deliberate, but it can read as teleporting on a first watch.
- **Not measured:** phone performance. The character is redrawn in Canvas2D every frame.
- **Three inks.** Crimson is used as a full ink (face, good soil), not as the hairline the house anchors prefer.
