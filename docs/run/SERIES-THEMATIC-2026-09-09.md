**Thematic:** What it looks like when God meets someone not with an answer but with rest, food, and a quiet voice, after they have decided they are the only one left and it would be easier to stop.

**Scripture spine candidate:** 1 Kings 19:1-18 (Elijah under the broom tree, at Horeb)

**Human condition:** Bone-deep, isolated exhaustion — the conviction that you are carrying this alone, that no one else is still holding on, and that quitting quietly would be reasonable rather than dramatic.

**Rationale:** Recent coverage of church culture keeps landing on fatigue rather than anger as the dominant note — pieces on burned-out believers "rethinking the church," 38% of church leaders having considered leaving ministry in the past year, and deconstruction increasingly described as downstream of burnout rather than doctrine. Parallel discourse on loneliness inside congregations ("Why Do We Feel Lonely at Church," "Feeling Alone in a Crowded Congregation") and on prayer that doesn't resolve anxiety ("why anxiety doesn't always go away when you pray") points to the same underlying ache: people who are still present, still praying, still showing up, but privately convinced they are the only one left standing and running on nothing. Elijah's story answers that condition point for point — collapse after victory, "I, even I only, am left," and a God who orders sleep and bread before he ever gets to the still small voice — which lets the series speak to exhaustion and isolation without naming the news cycle, and continues, without referencing, last week's ground of waiting in the stretch where nothing has arrived yet.
