> Elijah calls fire from heaven, wins the biggest public fight of his life — and the next day asks God to let him die. Seven days inside 1 Kings 19: the meal and sleep that come before a single word of counsel, the mountain where wind and earthquake and fire all get dismissed and a near-silence gets the visit, a question asked twice with the same answer both times, and a number — seven thousand — that turns out to be the real reply to "I am the only one left."

---

## Thematic rationale (from the discovery pass)

What it looks like when God meets someone not with an answer but with rest, food, and a quiet voice, after they have decided they are the only one left and it would be easier to stop.

Recent coverage of church culture keeps landing on fatigue rather than anger as the dominant note — pieces on burned-out believers "rethinking the church," church leaders considering leaving ministry, and deconstruction increasingly described as downstream of burnout rather than doctrine. Parallel discourse on loneliness inside congregations and on prayer that doesn't resolve anxiety points to the same underlying ache: people who are still present, still praying, still showing up, but privately convinced they are the only one left standing and running on nothing. Elijah's story answers that condition point for point — collapse after victory, "I, even I only, am left," and a God who orders sleep and bread before He ever gets to the still small voice. This series speaks to exhaustion and isolation without naming the news cycle or any statistic — the human condition is old enough that the text already answers it.

Each day 2-7 opens with a self-contained two-minute reading (scripture → vocab → teaching → reflection → prayer → DEEP DIVE) — a reader who stops there has had a whole devotional. The two-minute teaching for each day, so the shape of the week reads in under five minutes total:

**Day 2 — I Have Had Enough**

> Twenty-four hours before this scene, Elijah stood on Mount Carmel and watched fire fall from heaven on command, in front of the whole nation, ending a three-year drought and a religious war he'd been fighting mostly alone. It was the single greatest public victory of his life. Then Jezebel sends one threat, and he runs... Most readers expect collapse to follow failure. This collapse follows the biggest win of his career. The text doesn't explain that as strange. It just reports it, plainly — because exhaustion after triumph isn't a contradiction in the story of a person's life. It's often exactly when the body and the spirit finally stop performing and tell the truth about the cost.

**Day 3 — Get Up and Eat**

> Elijah has just asked God to let him die... And the first thing that happens to him is not a vision, not a rebuke, not even a question. An angel touches him and says two words: get up, eat... Whatever this week eventually says about the still small voice, it says this first: the voice waited. The meal didn't.

**Day 4 — Not in the Fire**

> God does not simply speak to Elijah on the mountain. He stages an entrance first. Wind that splits rock. An earthquake. A fire... Then comes the phrase everyone remembers, even in translation: a still small voice. It sounds gentle in English. The Hebrew is stranger than gentle... Instead, on the one day he actually needed to hear from God more than any other day of his life, God skipped every display he was famous for and spoke in something closer to silence.

**Day 5 — What Are You Doing Here**

> God has already asked this exact question once, before the wind and the earthquake and the fire... He gives the identical answer. Word for word... A reader might expect God to correct him here... That correction doesn't come — not yet, not directly. What comes instead is a set of instructions. Three names. A road to travel. A successor to anoint. God's answer to "I am alone" turns out not to be an argument at all. It's an assignment.

**Day 6 — Seven Thousand You Don't Know About**

> Twice, Elijah has said the same sentence: I am the only one left. Twice, God has let him say it without correction. Now, finally, comes the actual answer — and it isn't a rebuke, and it isn't reassurance in the soft, feelings-focused sense. It's a number... God's response to "I am alone" is not "you shouldn't feel that way." It's "here is what you didn't know."

**Day 7 — recap**

> That's the whole week, in order. Nothing about it argues him out of his exhaustion. Every bit of it takes his exhaustion completely seriously — and answers it anyway.

## Verification (references/verification-standards.md)

- All scripture pulled verbatim from the committed `public/bibles/` corpus — no other source. Casing preserved exactly.
- Four parallel research agents (stories / quotes / Hebrew+context / videos) fetched primary or scholarly-triangulated sources directly. Key findings that shaped the text:
  - **Rothem (the broom tree) is _Retama raetam_, a largely leafless desert shrub — not a juniper, not a canopy tree.** Carried into the vocab module, the drafting, and the (undelivered) art brief.
  - **דְּמָמָה (demamah) is one of the rarest nouns in the Old Testament** — occurs only in 1 Kings 19:12 and Job 4:16. "Still small voice" is presented honestly as a smoothing translation of a more literal, paradoxical Hebrew phrase.
  - The "fine as dust" golden-calf parallel is correctly cited to **Deuteronomy 9:21**, not Exodus 32:20 (a citation slip caught before drafting).
- Five stories, each independently checked: Spurgeon's own depression (primary lecture text, _Lectures to My Students_), Newton's care for Cowper (secondary scholarly citation of his diary, held with that caveat), Pascal's Memorial (primary Gutenberg translation), Colson/Prison Fellowship (memoir + interview), Brother Andrew (used to show the frequency of Elijah's exact false belief across many documented people, not as a tight personal parallel).
- Quotes verified verbatim against fetched primary sources: Spurgeon ("Elijah Fainting," "The Still Small Voice"), Alexander Maclaren ("Elijah's Weakness, and Its Cure," cross-checked across three independent digitizations), Dietrich Bonhoeffer (_Life Together_).
- **A full editorial review pass caught six citation-integrity issues** (quoted text that didn't match the source pack's abbreviated transcription) and two resource-module verses quoted in non-BSB wording. All eight were traced, corrected against the actual primary sources, and re-verified in a second editorial pass. None required cutting content — every flagged quote turned out to be genuinely verified text that the source pack had simply abbreviated; the pack was corrected to carry the full quotes.
- Three video slots (burnout, recommissioning, remnant) have no verified official-channel video in BibleProject's catalog for that exact angle — those days ship without an inline video rather than a mismatched substitute.
- Editorial repetition sweep (SA-032) applied: removed duplicate rhetorical openers between Day 2 and Day 5's parallel pivot positions, and thinned a "worth [gerund]" tic that had clustered in Day 6.

21 inline-image briefs (3 per day, botanically corrected — the broom tree specified as a low shrub, never a canopy) plus a series hero were drafted, then removed from the shipped day JSONs, because a missing-file image reference is a hard failure in `check-devotional-consistency.mjs` that blocks the pre-commit hook. **Fix:** refresh Codex auth (`codex login` on the founder's machine, then re-upload to the `CODEX_AUTH_JSON` secret for the cloud workflow), then generate the 22 masters and re-add the inline-image modules — the briefs are recoverable from this run's session log if needed, or can be re-derived from the day text.

### 2. Narration — BLOCKED, no Linux path in the founder-voice pipeline

The dry-run cost gate passed cleanly (97,258 characters against 257,194 available credits — well within budget) and rendering began. Every one of the 7 days failed at the audio-decode step: `FAILED: [Errno 2] No such file or directory: 'afconvert'`.

`afconvert` is a macOS-only Core Audio tool. **Every narration script in `euangelion-voice-prototype/spec/`** (`render_el_catalog.py`, `render_kokoro.py`, `render_chatterbox.py`, `mix_atmosphere.py`, `mine_channel.py`) hardcodes it with no ffmpeg fallback, despite this cloud environment provisioning ffmpeg specifically for this purpose. This means **the standing weekly-series automation likely cannot render narration on the Linux CI runner as currently written** — a plausible explanation for why the prior maiden-voyage attempt (`series/auto-2026-08-26`) never reached a PR. Credits for the first chunk of each of the 7 days were already spent and are cached in `euangelion-voice-prototype/.el-chunk-cache/` (confirmed 7 cached files) — a rerun after the fix is free. **Fix (not attempted here, out of scope for a content build):** add an ffmpeg-backed code path to `decode()` and the two `afconvert` encode calls in `render_el_catalog.py`, matching `afconvert`'s exact output contract (mono LEI16 WAVE for decode; AAC 64kbps mono m4a for the no-music encode fallback).

### 3. Build — BLOCKED, missing client-config secrets

`npm run build` fails at `check-public-config.mjs` (SA-102, deliberately unbypassable): `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY` and `NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_URL` are not available in this environment (only `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL` and `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY` are). No fake value was substituted — SA-102 exists specifically to catch a build that would otherwise silently ship with no working Supabase client. `npm run preview` and live route verification (Phase 12) could not run for the same reason.
